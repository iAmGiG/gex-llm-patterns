\section{Discussion}

\subsection{What Reproduces, and What the Agreement Means}
\label{sec:discussion:what}

% src: Section 4.1 (Table 2), Section 4.2 (oss 99.6 percent against o4-mini on 1,412 windows; 99.7 percent with the rule on the complete series), Section 4.4 (qwen_full_spy agree_pct 98.7 on 4,206 answered windows)
The evaluation can be reproduced after its hosted model is gone, with a limit. On the stored requests, gpt-oss-20b at a pinned revision gives the stored verdict of the retired model in 99.6\% of 1{,}412 windows, and agrees with the rule in 99.7\% of the windows of the complete series; Qwen3-8B does the same on the windows that it answers. The route does not depend on the hosted successor, which is the point of rung~3 in Figure~\ref{fig:ladder}: the hosted alias also reproduces these verdicts (Paper A), but it has no dated snapshot and cannot be run again if it is retired. The limit is that a model has to be able to do the task. Llama~3.1~8B with the verdict first cannot, and its constant answer agrees with the rule only where the rule's own verdict is nearly constant.

Because the prompt states the rule, what the agreement measures is rule-following on stated criteria, which is the object of this paper and nothing more. It shows that the model's verdict equals the rule's on a window; it does not show that the model reasons about dealer gamma without the criteria, and these data cannot separate applying the three thresholds to 30 rounded values from recall of the series, which obfuscation of dates and tickers only makes less likely. This is why the task is a useful reproducibility target: it separates a replacement that works from one that does not window by window, and it exposes one that gives plausible text for a wrong answer, as the Llama~3.1~8B answers do.

\subsection{Where the Models Depart, and Why That Matters}
\label{sec:discussion:depart}

The departures from the rule are not noise: they are one-sided, towards detection, and they sit at the threshold that is hardest to compute, the number of sign changes in 30 values. A reader who replaces the hosted model by an open-weights model of this size should expect agreement of about 95\% to 100\% where it answers, and the remaining windows to fail the flip limit by one or two sign changes. We have no evidence on larger or other models.

\subsection{Non-Answers as a Cost of Reproduction}
\label{sec:discussion:nonanswer}

A model that reasons at length can run out of tokens before it writes its verdict. Qwen3-8B did so on 8.8\% of the windows, mostly windows that the rule rejects, so a rate over the answered windows overstates the detection rate, and a reproduction that reports only agreement on answered windows looks better than it is. We report the unanswered windows and bound the rate; the bounds are wide, 14.5\% to 35.1\% for 2024 and 2025, and we claim no point estimate. A follow-up on stored data could recover verdicts from answers cut off after the verdict field had been written, which would narrow the bounds without a new model call; we have not done it and report no number for it.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion:limits}

% src: runs.csv.gz (condensed/) run_metadata (no sampling parameters stored; local.engine_args, local.model_revision, local.vllm_version, local.gpu stored); planning/02_decisions_log.md D-29 to D-32; Table 1 (generation_config.json defaults); bridge_agreement.csv comparison=test_retest (same-job repeats)
The comparison is limited by its design. (i)~Three models of 8 to 20 billion parameters, one engine version, one GPU type and one prompt are a small sample. (ii)~The hosted alias has no dated snapshot and cannot be run again, so every hosted number here is cited from Paper A. (iii)~The sampling defaults differ by model (temperature 1.0, 0.6 and 0.6; top-p and top-k differ) and are not stored in the run metadata of these runs; they come from the repository files and the engine logs, and the decoding control covers two models on one arm. (iv)~The repeats ran in the same job and cover 5\% of the windows, so they measure variation within a job, not drift across jobs, GPUs or engine versions. (v)~Windows have stride one, so agreement and kappa carry no interval, and contrasts rest on a quarter-block bootstrap or an anti-conservative McNemar test. (vi)~All Qwen3-8B results are conditional on answering, and its protocol was amended after the failed pilot gate (Section~\ref{sec:results:qwen}). (vii)~GPU hours are summed engine wall-clock time on shared GPUs. (viii)~The task is one rule on one asset; agreement with it says nothing about other tasks.
