\subsection{What Reproduces, and What the Agreement Means}
\label{sec:discussion:what}

% src: unanswered_bounds_with_recoverable.csv arm=qwen_full_spy period=2024-2025 (lower_pct 14.541833, upper_pct 35.059761, lower_rec_pct 14.541833, upper_rec_pct 32.868526); unanswered_answering_logit.csv model=qwen3-8b scope=full_spy term=rule_detects (odds_ratio 15.844448); Section 4.1 (Table 2), Section 4.2 (oss 99.6 percent against o4-mini on 1,412 windows; 99.7 percent with the rule on the complete series), Section 4.4 (qwen_full_spy agree_pct 98.7 on 4,206 answered windows; unanswered_agreement_variants.csv arm=qwen_full_spy period=all agree_pct_unans_error 88.488595)
The evaluation can be reproduced after its hosted model is gone, with a limit. On the stored requests, gpt-oss-20b at a pinned revision gives the stored verdict of the retired model in 99.6\% of 1{,}412 windows, and agrees with the rule in 99.7\% of the windows of the complete series; Qwen3-8B agrees with it in 98.7\% of the windows that it answers, and in 88.5\% of all windows of the series when its unanswered windows count as errors. The route does not depend on the hosted successor, which is the point of rung~3 in Figure~\ref{fig:ladder}: the hosted alias also reproduces these verdicts (Paper A), but it has no dated snapshot and cannot be run again if it is retired. The limit is that a model has to be able to do the task. Llama~3.1~8B with the verdict first cannot, and its constant answer agrees with the rule only where the rule's own verdict is nearly constant.

Because the prompt states the rule, what the agreement measures is rule-following on stated criteria, which is the object of this paper and nothing more. It shows that the model's verdict equals the rule's on a window; it does not show that the model reasons about dealer gamma without the criteria, and these data cannot separate applying the three thresholds to 30 rounded values from recall of the series, which obfuscation of dates and tickers only makes less likely. This is why the task is a useful reproducibility target: it separates a replacement that works from one that does not window by window, and it exposes one that gives plausible text for a wrong answer, as the Llama~3.1~8B answers do.

\subsection{Where the Models Depart, and Why That Matters}
\label{sec:discussion:depart}

The departures from the rule are not noise: they are one-sided, towards detection, and they sit most often at the flip limit, the count of sign changes in 30 values, which these models report least reliably. A reader who replaces the hosted model by an open-weights model of this size should expect, on these arms, agreement of about 95\% to 100\% where it answers, and the remaining windows to fail the flip limit by one or two sign changes.

\subsection{Non-Answers as a Cost of Reproduction}
\label{sec:discussion:nonanswer}

A model that reasons at length can run out of tokens before it writes its verdict. Qwen3-8B did so on 8.8\% of the windows, mostly windows that the rule rejects, so a rate over the answered windows likely overstates the detection rate, and a reproduction that reports only agreement on answered windows looks better than it is (the two figures for Qwen3-8B are in Section~\ref{sec:discussion:what}). We report the unanswered windows and bound the rate. The bounds for 2024 and 2025 stay wide even with the verdicts that cut answers already hold (Section~\ref{sec:results:qwen}), and we claim no point estimate. Which windows go unanswered is associated with the window, most strongly with whether the rule detects it, so the non-answers are not a random loss.

\subsection{Answer Order and Sampling: What Is Known}
\label{sec:discussion:order}

The runs of Sections~\ref{sec:results:order} and~\ref{sec:results:repeat} bear on two explanations and leave a third open. With the reasoning first, Llama~3.1~8B no longer says yes to every window, so the verdict-first order explains its near-constant answer. It still does not follow the rule, and its reported counts are almost never right, so schema order does not explain the failure of the task; what does is left open (Section~\ref{sec:results:llama}). The decoding control argues against sampling noise at the repository defaults as the explanation of the two results on the published inputs: at temperature 0, gpt-oss-20b still agrees with the rule in 99.7\% of the windows it answers and Llama~3.1~8B still detects all 1{,}476 windows. That holds for this arm and these two models only.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion:limits}

% src: runs.csv.gz (condensed/) run_metadata (no sampling parameters stored; local.engine_args, local.model_revision, local.vllm_version, local.gpu stored); planning/02_decisions_log.md D-29 to D-32; Table 1 (generation_config.json defaults); bridge_agreement.csv comparison=test_retest (same-job repeats)
The comparison is limited by its design. (i)~Three models of 8 to 20 billion parameters, one engine version, one GPU type and one prompt are a small sample. (ii)~The hosted alias has no dated snapshot and cannot be run again, so every hosted number here is cited from Paper A. (iii)~The sampling defaults differ by model (temperature 1.0, 0.6 and 0.6; top-p and top-k differ) and are not stored in the run metadata of these runs; they come from the repository files and the engine logs, and the decoding control covers two models on one arm; the variants record the sampling they send, the three models do not. (iv)~The repeats ran in the same job and cover 5\% of the windows, so they measure variation within a job, not drift across jobs, GPUs or engine versions. (v)~Windows have stride one, so agreement and kappa carry no interval, and contrasts rest on a quarter-block bootstrap or an anti-conservative McNemar test. (vi)~All Qwen3-8B results are conditional on answering (88.5\% agreement with the rule on the complete series if the unanswered windows count as errors), and its protocol was amended after the failed pilot gate (Section~\ref{sec:results:qwen}). (vii)~GPU hours are the engine time of the runs, summed; the elapsed time is shorter because runs overlapped on several shared GPUs (31.7 hours for Qwen3-8B against 98.5 summed), and both depend on the load of other users. (viii)~The task is one rule on one asset; agreement with it says nothing about other tasks.
