\subsection{Llama 3.1 8B with the Verdict First Does Not Apply the Criteria}
\label{sec:results:llama}

% src: cost_ledger.csv model=llama-3.1-8b-instruct (n_requests summed 12,378); runs.csv.gz run_metadata.local.seconds (summed 11,904.2 s = 3.31 h); llm_parse_stats.csv model=llama-3.1-8b-instruct (n_parsed = n_rows = 11,750; n_finish_length 0; mean_completion_tokens 169.9 on the seven arms); detection_by_year.csv label_source=llm scope=all arm=llama_* (n_detect 1,476 / 4,690 / 4,690 / 223 x 4; agree_pct; kappa 0.000 to 0.000064); bridge_agreement.csv comparison=bridge ref_arm=r1_p4_o4mini other_arm=llama_p34_dated_2020 (27, 0, 196, 0)
Llama~3.1~8B answers the seven arms with 12{,}378 requests in 3.3 GPU hours, every answer parses at the first attempt, and the answers are short (170 completion tokens on average). But its verdicts carry no information about the window: it detects a regime in all 1{,}476 windows of the published inputs, in all 223 windows of each baseline arm and in 4{,}690 of the 4{,}691 windows of each complete series. Its agreement with the rule therefore equals the detection rate of the rule, with kappa 0.000 in every arm (Table~\ref{tab:results}). On the published inputs it detects all 501 windows of 2024 and 2025, as o4-mini did for the 486 that it answered, but it also detects all 223 windows of the 2020 baseline, where o4-mini detected 27 (stored answers of the earlier runs \cite{regan2026regimes,regan2026rerun}), so its 2024-versus-2020 contrast is zero. A detection rate of 100\% on a sample is what a constant answer gives; the contrast with a sample in which few windows qualify is the part of the test that a model has to pass, and Llama~3.1~8B does not pass it.

The numbers that it reports do not follow the window either: its reported mean $|\mathrm{GEX}|$ lies within 5\% of the value computed from the window in 14.3\% to 64.1\% of the answers per arm (at least 98.4\% for gpt-6-luna, from Paper A), and its sign-flip count is exact in 2.7\% to 13.9\%. The reasoning field argues for the verdict with comparisons that are false. For a window of \ident{full_spy} ending 2008-02-27 it reports 10 positive and 20 negative days and writes that ``the persistence percentage of 66.67\% meets the 70\% threshold'' and that ``the average magnitude of \$2.45B is greater than the \$5B threshold''. Table~S5 lists these measures for each arm.

\paragraph{What is and is not known about why.}
Known: the model detects almost every window whatever the window is; its working is written after the verdict, under a schema that puts the verdict first (Section~\ref{sec:methods:schema}); and the working is not faithful to the window, since it states comparisons that its own counts contradict. Not known: the cause. A model that commits to a verdict before any working may answer yes because the first token has no evidence behind it; but an 8B model may also be unable to count the signs of 30 values and compare the count with a threshold in either order, or may default to ``yes'' on a prompt that lists criteria.
