\subsection{Qwen3-8B Follows the Rule Where It Answers, and Leaves Windows Unanswered}
\label{sec:results:qwen}

% src: llm_parse_stats.csv model=qwen3-8b arm_role=primary (n_rows 11,750; n_parsed 10,718; n_finish_length 1,032; mean_completion_tokens 5,368 row-weighted on the seven arms) and gpt-6-luna arms (758, Paper A); cost_ledger.csv model=qwen3-8b (n_requests summed 22,400; first pass 12,378; the retry runs run_metadata.retry_of 10,022); runs.csv.gz run_metadata.local.seconds (summed 354,438.9 s = 98.5 h); missing_answer_bounds.csv period=all arm=qwen_* (n_unparsed summed 1,032, n_unparsed_clf_no_detect summed 1,011; qwen_full_spy parsed_pct_clf_detect 98.850575, parsed_pct_clf_no_detect 88.290054); planning/02_decisions_log.md D-32 (pilot 36 of 40 parsed against the 95 percent gate; amendment before the arms); unanswered_gpu_time.csv model=qwen3-8b (hours_sum 98.455, union_hours 31.669, n_gpus 4); unanswered_census_unanswered.csv model=qwen3-8b arm_role=primary kind (missing 884 = 17 + 867, failed 147 = 4 + 143, complete_at_cap 1); unanswered_recoverable_verdicts.csv population=final_unanswered_primary model=qwen3-8b (n_rows summed 1,032, n_with_text 148, n_cut_after_verdict 144, n_cut_before_verdict 3, n_complete 1, n_verdict 145, n_verdict_agrees_rule 143); unanswered_recoverable_first_pass_vs_retry.csv model=qwen3-8b (n 316, n_same 313)
Qwen3-8B reasons at great length: its answers average 5{,}368 completion tokens on the seven arms, against 758 for gpt-6-luna on the same arms (Paper A). Of its 12{,}378 first-pass requests, 10{,}022 reached the limit of 4{,}000 tokens and were requested again at 8{,}000, so its arms sent 22{,}400 requests, which took 98.5 GPU hours of summed engine time (about 31.7 hours elapsed, on four GPUs; Section~\ref{sec:results:cost}). After the retry, 10{,}718 of the 11{,}750 windows of the seven arms (91.2\%) hold a parsed answer; each of the other 1{,}032 (8.8\%) ended at the limit of 8{,}000 tokens without one. In 884 of them the model left no JSON text at all; 148 hold JSON text, 144 of them cut off after the verdict field, 3 before it and 1 a complete object that reached the limit. The verdict is therefore readable in 145 of the 1{,}032 cut answers, and it equals the verdict of the rule in 143 of the 145 (Table~S7). For the 316 windows whose cut first answer holds a verdict and whose retry parsed, the two verdicts are the same in 313. The 40-window pilot had parsed 36 answers, short of the 95\% that the protocol required; before the arms ran we amended the protocol (decision D-32, disclosed) to run them unchanged and to report such windows as unanswered, so the cap was kept by design after the gate failed, not fixed in advance; every statement about Qwen3-8B below is conditional on its answering.

% src: unanswered_answering_logit.csv model=qwen3-8b scope=full_spy (auc 0.744327; odds_ratio rule_detects 15.844448); unanswered_answering_bands.csv model=qwen3-8b scope=full_spy (variable=persistence_pct band=70-<80 answered_pct 79.769392; variable=sign_flips bands 0-1 and 8-10, answered_pct 100.0 and 84.179357); unanswered_repeat_stability.csv model=qwen3-8b (n_repeated 588, n_both 483, n_neither 13, n_one_pass_only 92)
The unanswered windows are not a random subset. 1{,}011 of the 1{,}032 are windows that the rule rejects: on the complete series Qwen3-8B answers 98.9\% of the windows that the rule detects but 88.3\% of those that it rejects (Table~S6). Whether a window is answered depends on the window: the share answered is lowest at a persistence of 70\% to 80\% (79.8\%) and falls with the number of sign flips (100.0\% at 0 or 1 flips, 84.2\% at 8 to 10 and 85.5\% at 11 or more), and in a logistic model of answering a window that the rule detects has about 16 times the odds of being answered, the other terms held fixed (odds ratio 15.8; in-sample area under the curve 0.744; Table~S9). Answering is also not repeatable: of the 588 windows requested twice, 92 were answered in one pass only. A rate over the answered windows would therefore likely overstate the detection rate, and we report bounds: the lower bound counts every unanswered window as not detected and the upper bound as detected (Figure~\ref{fig:rate}).

Where it answers, Qwen3-8B follows the rule: it agrees with the rule in 98.7\% of the answered windows of the complete series (kappa 0.948) and in 99.4\% without same-day expiries (kappa 0.973), and with gpt-6-luna in 98.5\% (kappa 0.941). This share depends on how the 485 unanswered windows of the series are treated: counted as not detected, the agreement is 98.7\% of all 4{,}691 windows (4{,}629), but counted as errors it is 88.5\% (4{,}151; kappa is not defined for this count, Table~S8). The 98.7\% is thus a statement about the answered windows only. On the 521 contested windows of the complete series (Section~\ref{sec:methods:statistics}) it answers 454 (87.1\%) and agrees with the rule on 92.3\% of them (80.4\% of the 521 if the unanswered ones count as errors), against 99.5\% of the 3{,}752 windows it answers elsewhere; gpt-oss-20b agrees on 98.3\% of all 521 and gpt-6-luna on 97.5\% (Paper A \cite{regan2026rerun}). Over four margin sets its agreement on the contested windows that it answers lies between 92.3\% and 97.5\% (Table~S10). On the published inputs it gives the verdict of o4-mini in 1{,}273 of the 1{,}287 stored verdicts that it answered (98.9\%, kappa 0.978), and it answers and detects all 501 windows of 2024 and 2025.

The bounds are wide where many windows are unanswered. On the 399 windows of the complete series from 2024 and 2025 that it answered it detects 73 and the rule 57; over all 502 windows, 103 of them unanswered, its rate lies between 14.5\% and 35.1\%, against 12.0\% for the rule and 12.5\% for gpt-6-luna (Paper A); without same-day expiries the bounds are 15.9\% and 23.3\%, against 14.7\% for the rule (Paper A \cite{regan2026rerun}). Eleven of the 103 unanswered windows hold a verdict in the cut answer, and all eleven are not detected; counting them narrows the bounds to 14.5\% and 32.9\% (15.9\% and 21.7\% without same-day expiries; Table~S8). The wide upper end remains, because 92 windows have no readable verdict. In the 2024 baseline the dates change nothing that the data can detect: on the 190 windows answered under both labelings it detects 178 with relative labels and 182 with dates (quarter-block bootstrap $p=0.125$). With dates, however, it answers fewer of the windows that the rule rejects (19 of 42, against 33 with relative labels) and detects 9 of the 19, which makes this arm its largest departure from the rule (kappa 0.668). Its 2024-versus-2020 contrast is at least 70.0 percentage points with relative labels and 69.5 with dates (lower bound for 2024 minus upper bound for 2020), so it passes the part of the test that Llama~3.1~8B fails.
