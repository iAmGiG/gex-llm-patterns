\label{sec:results:repeat}

% src: bridge_agreement.csv comparison=test_retest scope=all other_arm=<arm>#rep1 (n_pairs, agree_pct): oss_* 74/74, 234/235, 241/241 (six repeats added by a second generation step), 11/11 x 4 = 593 of 594; llama_* 74, 235, 235, 11 x 4 = 588 of 588; qwen_* 57/57, 193/195, 196/197, 7/7, 5/5, 11/11, 11/11 = 480 of 483 pairs answered in both passes; condensed/detections.csv.gz arms <arm>#rep1 for the 588 requested repeats of Qwen3-8B (530 parsed, 483 with both answers parsed); bridge_agreement.csv comparison=test_retest ref_arm=bridge_jan other_arm=bridge_jan#rep1 (n_pairs 1476, agree_pct 99.254743; Paper A)
Each arm carries a sample of windows that was requested twice in the same job (Section~\ref{sec:methods:repeats}). The repeat returns the first verdict in 593 of the 594 windows of gpt-oss-20b, and in all 588 windows of Llama~3.1~8B, whose answers are constant. For Qwen3-8B, 483 of the 588 repeated windows were answered in both passes, and in 480 of these the verdict is the same; the 105 other windows were unanswered in at least one pass, so that the retest of Qwen3-8B is conditional on answering, too. For comparison, a full repeat of the 1{,}476 windows of \ident{bridge_jan} with the hosted alias returned the first verdict in 99.3\% (Paper A). The pinned models are therefore no less repeatable than the hosted one on these samples, at the sampling defaults of their repositories. The repeats ran in the same job as the first requests, so they bound the variation within a job; they say nothing about drift between jobs, weeks apart, on other GPUs or in other engine versions, which we did not test.
