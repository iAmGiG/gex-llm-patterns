\subsection{Does the Order of the Schema Explain the Llama Failure?}
\label{sec:results:order}

% src: exploratory_llama_reorder_20261108/output.jsonl (local, git-ignored, kept with the deposit: 40 answers, regime_detected true in 32, equal to clf_should_detect in 26; confusion table with clf_should_detect: 7 and 13 for the 20 rejected windows, 1 and 19 for the 20 detected ones); condensed/detections.csv.gz arm=pilot_llama_bridge_jan (40 answers on the same 40 windows: regime_detected true in 40, equal to clf_should_detect in 20), arm=pilot_llamarf_bridge_jan (27 detected, 27 equal to clf_should_detect); condensed/windows.csv.gz series_key=jan2026_phase4a clf_should_detect (20 of the 40 windows); planning/02_decisions_log.md D-30 (exploratory run, pilot), D-33
Two runs bear on this question. On 2026-11-08, after the 40-window pilot, we sent the same 40 requests to Llama~3.1~8B with the schema reordered so that the reasoning and the counts come before the verdict (Appendix~\ref{app:schema}). That run is outside the stored arms: it was a single exploratory pass, and its answers are kept with the deposit and not in the result tables. The 40 windows are balanced on the rule (20 detected, 20 not). With the verdict first the model detected all 40 and agreed with the rule in 20; with the reasoning first it detected 32 and agreed in 26. The order changed the verdicts and moved them towards the rule, but not far: 13 of the 20 windows that the rule rejects were still detected, and 1 of the 20 that it detects was missed.

The full run (decision D-33) repeats the seven arms with the reasoning field first, and the model no longer says yes to every window. Every window is answered (one answer needed the retry at 8{,}000 tokens), and it detects 47.1\% of the windows of the complete series (2{,}210 of 4{,}691; the rule 13.0\%) and 65.9\% of the published inputs (972 of 1{,}476; the rule 45.2\%); in 2024 and 2025 it detects 279 of the 502 windows, against 60 for the rule. It still does not follow the rule. Its agreement is 56.7\% on the complete series (kappa 0.096), 67.0\% on the published inputs (kappa 0.360) and 35.4\% to 54.7\% on the four baseline arms (kappa 0.048 to 0.092); against the stored o4-mini verdicts it agrees in 67.2\% of 1{,}412 windows (kappa 0.361). Table~\ref{tab:order} lists every arm. The numbers that it reports are wrong: its reported mean $|\mathrm{GEX}|$ lies within 5\% of the computed value in 14.8\% to 38.7\% of the answers per arm, and its reported metrics as a whole equal the computed ones in less than 0.5\% of them.

The two runs say the same. The verdict-first order explains why the \ident{llama_} arms answer yes to almost every window: with the reasoning first the verdict is no longer constant. It does not explain the failure of the task: the reasoning-first verdicts are close to chance (kappa about 0.1 on the complete series) and its working is wrong, so schema order is not the whole explanation, and this run does not say what else is (Section~\ref{sec:discussion:order}).
