\subsection{What the Published Inputs Were}
\label{sec:results:inputs}

Table~\ref{tab:lineage} compares the inputs recovered from the Phase 4A batch file with each candidate series, and Figures~\ref{fig:window} and~\ref{fig:series} show one window and the 2008--2026 history.
% src: batch file date 2026-01-04: planning/forensics/2026-10-28_memo_published_inputs.md and scripts/validation/electronics/README.md line 10; windows.csv.gz series_key=jan2026_phase4a (count, min and max window_end); series_comparison.csv pair jan2026_phase4a_vs_new_full scope=all n_days; report.yaml diagnostics.flagged_windows (jan2026_phase4a n_missing_master_days); missing dates: series parquet jan2026_phase4a_SPY against new_full_SPY
The batch file of 2026-01-04 stores the daily values that were sent to o4-mini. They give 1{,}476 windows with ends from 2020-02-13 to 2025-12-31, cut from 1{,}505 daily values. Compared with the complete series, the January calendar lacks 2023-07-12 and 2024-03-11. The 58 windows that span one of the two gaps are flagged and kept (Section~\ref{sec:methods:windows}).

% src: series_comparison.csv pairs jan2026_phase4a_vs_new_full and call_only_vs_jan2026_phase4a, exact_match_pct by year (Table lineage); CALL_FROM and the isolated days: series parquet jan2026_phase4a_SPY, call_only_SPY and new_full_SPY at the 0.01B precision (tolerance 0.005B)
The published inputs follow two different series (Table~\ref{tab:lineage}). In 2020 to 2022 the share of daily values that equal \ident{new_full} at the 0.01B precision of the prompt is 100.0\%, 99.2\% and 98.4\%, and the call-only series equals the inputs on at most 1.2\% of the days. In 2024 and 2025 the relation is reversed: none of the values equals \ident{new_full} (0.0\% in both years) and every value equals the call-only GEX (100.0\% in both years). The year 2023 is the transition: 60.6\% of its values equal \ident{new_full} and 39.4\% equal the call-only series, which together account for every day. From 2023-09-29 on, every value equals the call-only GEX; the day before, 2023-09-28, is the last on which a value differs from it. Before that date, 39 days (the first on 2021-09-17) already equal the call-only value and not the complete one. The published inputs of 2024 and 2025 are therefore the call leg of the options chain without the put leg, and they differ in level and sign from the complete series: the mean $|\mathrm{GEX}|$ of the published inputs is \$20.19~billion in 2024 against \$6.48~billion on the complete series, and \$19.21~billion against \$7.13~billion in 2025, with the same daily sign on 62.2\% and 54.0\% of the days.

% src: series_comparison.csv pair old_bs_vs_jan2026_phase4a (exact_match_pct, sign_agree_pct, mean_abs_a_bn) years 2020-2025; missing dates: series parquet old_bs_SPY against new_full_SPY
The pre-rebuild archive (\ident{old_bs}) was computed from the same put-less table for the same dates, so it cannot serve as a clean earlier convention. Its Black--Scholes gamma differs from the stored gamma, and none of its values equals a published value at 0.01B (0.0\% in every year). Its signs agree with the published inputs on 100.0\% of the days in 2024 and 99.6\% in 2025, and its mean $|\mathrm{GEX}|$ is \$16.19~billion and \$14.71~billion, which is the level of a call-only series. The archive also has holes: it lacks 33 days of the complete calendar (2023-07-12, 2024-03-11, the 26 days from 2026-01-02 to 2026-02-09, and the 5 days from 2026-10-05 to 2026-10-09).

% src: series parquet p3_2024_SPY and p4_2020_SPY (252 values each; sign counts, positive-value range, mean absolute value); windows.csv.gz series_key=p3_2024 and p4_2020 (223 windows each)
The inputs of the Phase 3 and 4 runs of November 2025 survive in the same way: 252 daily values for 2024 and 252 for 2020, 223 windows each, with ISO dates in the prompts. The 2024 values are a gross call-plus-put magnitude with a negative sign imposed. They are negative on 242 days, and the 10 positive values (\$10.61~billion to \$13.76~billion) are placeholder rows of the source database. The 2020 values are a magnitude only and are positive on every day. The mean absolute daily value is \$31.24~billion for 2024 and \$2.85~billion for 2020, so the two baselines differ in convention and in scale (Section~\ref{sec:results:dated}).

\subsection{Classifier Replay by Year}
\label{sec:results:replay}

Table~\ref{tab:replay} and Figure~\ref{fig:replay} give the detection rate of the deterministic classifier by year for each series next to the published o4-mini rates, and Figure~\ref{fig:thresholds} shows how far the replay depends on the thresholds.
% src: stats.yaml rates arm=r1_o4mini periods 2024, 2025 (wilson_ci_pct); report.yaml headline.llm_vs_classifier_all_years arm=r1_o4mini (agree_pct, kappa, n_compared); detection_by_year.csv (classifier rows by series_key and year; arm r1_o4mini); stats.yaml rates (Wilson, bootstrap_quarter, nonoverlap_30d) and contrasts series_contrast period 2024-2025 and period_shift
On the recovered inputs the rule reproduces the published labels in 1{,}412 of 1{,}412 parsed windows (100.0\%; Cohen's kappa 1.000), so the published labels follow from the published inputs and the published criteria. On the published inputs the rule detects 251 of 251 (100.0\%) of the windows ending in 2024 and 250 of 250 (100.0\%) in 2025, and the published o4-mini answers give 241 of 241 (100.0\%) and 245 of 245 (100.0\%) (Wilson 95\% lower bound 98.4\% in 2024 and 98.5\% in 2025). The call-only series and the archive give the same saturation (100.0\% in both years on the call-only series and 100.0\% on the archive). On the complete series the rule detects 26 of 252 (10.3\%) in 2024 (Wilson 95\% interval 7.1--14.7\%; quarter-block bootstrap 3.2--17.3\%, 4 blocks) and 34 of 250 (13.6\%) in 2025 (Wilson 95\% interval 9.9--18.4\%; bootstrap 2.3--25.4\%). Without same-day expiries the rates are 13.1\% and 16.4\%.

Pooled over 2024 and 2025 the detection rate is 100.0\% on the published inputs (501 of 501 windows) and 12.0\% on the complete series (60 of 502; Wilson 9.4--15.1\%, quarter-block bootstrap 5.0--19.2\%; non-overlapping subsample 3 of 17, and between 5.9\% and 25.0\% over the 30 possible starting windows). The paired difference is 88.0 percentage points (quarter-block bootstrap interval 80.8--95.0, $p=5.0\times10^{-4}$, the smallest value that 2{,}000 draws can give; exact McNemar test on the non-overlapping subsample $p=1.2\times10^{-4}$).

The period contrast changes sign. On the published inputs the rule detects 17.0\% of the windows of 2020 to 2023 and 100.0\% of those of 2024 and 2025, a shift of 83.0 percentage points ($\varphi=0.789$; bootstrap $p=5.0\times10^{-4}$; Fisher exact test on the non-overlapping subsample $p=2.7\times10^{-9}$). On the complete series the rates are 22.2\% and 12.0\%, a shift of $-$10.2 percentage points ($\varphi=-0.124$; bootstrap $p=0.108$; Fisher exact test on the subsample $p=0.728$): the complete series shows no increase after 2023 that the data distinguish from zero. In 2020 to 2023 the two series are of the same order (Table~\ref{tab:replay}).

% src: figures/output/fig12_threshold_sensitivity.json extra.rates (series_key jan2026_phase4a and new_full, rate_pct over 45 configurations) and extra.gap_pp_published_minus_rebuilt
Across the 45 threshold sets the rate on the published inputs stays at 100.0\%, whereas the rate on the complete series runs from 4.0\% to 29.3\%. The gap between the two is at least 70.7 and at most 96.0 percentage points in every configuration, so the difference between the published and the complete inputs does not depend on the exact thresholds.

\subsection{Model Agreement on the Published Inputs}
\label{sec:results:bridge}

Table~\ref{tab:bridge} and Figure~\ref{fig:bridge} compare gpt-6-luna with the published o4-mini answers on identical inputs.
% src: bridge_agreement.csv comparison=bridge ref_arm=r1_o4mini other_arm=bridge_jan scope=all and by year (n_pairs, confusion counts, agree_pct, kappa, bootstrap intervals, p_mcnemar_exact, p_boot); comparison=bridge_incl_recovered; comparison=test_retest ref_arm=bridge_jan; report.yaml headline.bridge.gate
The agreement gate of Section~\ref{sec:methods:model} (Cohen's kappa of at least 0.6) is met with a wide margin. On the 1{,}412 windows with a published answer the two models give the same verdict in 1{,}407 windows (99.6\%; quarter-block bootstrap interval 99.4--99.9\%), and kappa is 0.993 (bootstrap interval 0.986--0.998). The confusion matrix has 646 windows detected by both, 761 by neither, 0 only by o4-mini and 5 only by gpt-6-luna. In each of the 5 windows of disagreement the deterministic rule agrees with o4-mini. The windows lie in 2021 to 2023 (3 in 2021, 1 in 2022, 1 in 2023); the yearly kappa is lowest in 2021 (0.851) and between 0.986 and 1.000 in 2020, 2022 and 2023, and it is undefined in 2024 and 2025, where both models label every window as detected. The detection rates are 45.8\% for o4-mini and 46.1\% for gpt-6-luna on these windows. The paired McNemar test on all windows at stride one gives $p=0.063$ (anti-conservative, because the windows overlap) and the quarter-block bootstrap $p=0.010$ (Table~\ref{tab:bridge} gives both). No McNemar test on the non-overlapping subsample was run for this comparison.

% src: bridge_agreement.csv comparison=bridge_incl_recovered; llm_parse_stats.csv arm=r1_o4mini (n_recovered_2026)

\subsection{gpt-6-luna on the Complete Series}
\label{sec:results:corrected}

Figure~\ref{fig:corrected} shows the detection rate of gpt-6-luna by year on each SPY series, from the published inputs to the complete series, next to the classifier, and Table~\ref{tab:corrected} gives the yearly rates of the two complete series with their intervals.
% src: detection_by_year.csv label_source=llm arms full_spy, ex0dte_spy, bridge_jan, bridge_old (scope=all and by year); stats.yaml rates and contrasts (series_contrast llm, period 2024-2025); llm_parse_stats.csv; disagreements.csv
On the complete series gpt-6-luna labels 613 of 4{,}671 windows as persistent (13.1\%; 606 of them by the rule, 13.0\%) and, without same-day expiries, 494 of 4{,}671 (10.6\%; rule 491, 10.5\%). In 2024 and 2025 it detects 28 of 252 (11.1\%) and 35 of 250 (14.0\%) of the windows of the complete series (Wilson 7.8--15.6\% and 10.2--18.8\%) and 14.3\% and 16.4\% of those without same-day expiries. Pooled over the two years the rate is 12.5\% (63 of 502; Wilson 9.9--15.7\%, quarter-block bootstrap 6.1--19.6\%, non-overlapping subsample 3 of 17). The same model on the published inputs detects 100.0\% of the windows of these two years (501 of 501), and on the pre-rebuild archive 100.0\% (501 of 501). The paired difference between the published inputs and the complete series is 87.4 percentage points (quarter-block bootstrap interval 80.3--93.9, $p=5.0\times10^{-4}$; exact McNemar test on the non-overlapping subsample $p=1.2\times10^{-4}$), and that between the archive and the complete series is 87.4 percentage points ($p=5.0\times10^{-4}$ and $p=1.2\times10^{-4}$). In 2020 to 2023 the model detects 22.7\% of the windows of the complete series. \subsection{0DTE Ablation}
\label{sec:results:ablation}

Table~\ref{tab:ablation} separates the contribution of same-day expiries by comparing \ident{new_full} with \ident{new_ex0dte}.
% src: ex0dte_decomposition.csv (symbol SPY, QQQ, IWM; scope year; abs_0dte_share_pct, sign_flip_by_0dte_pct, rate_full_pct, rate_ex0dte_pct, n_full_only, n_ex0dte_only, llm_rate_*); stats.yaml contrasts series_contrast new_full versus new_ex0dte (paired by window end) for periods 2008-2019, 2020-2023, 2024, 2025, 2024-2025, 2026

\subsection{Cross-Asset Replication and the Floor Effect}
\label{sec:results:cross}

Table~\ref{tab:cross} and Figure~\ref{fig:cross} repeat the analysis for QQQ and IWM. The fixed dollar thresholds of Section~\ref{sec:methods:criteria} can produce a floor effect for smaller underlyings, which we report; the scaled series of Section~\ref{sec:methods:arms} show whether the model's verdict still follows the criteria when the floor is lifted.
% src: report.yaml diagnostics.floor_effect (max_window_mean_abs_gex_bn) and diagnostics.floor_effect_note; detection_by_year.csv scope=all (QQQ, IWM, SPY); windows.csv.gz (clf_persistence_pct, clf_sign_flips, clf_avg_magnitude)
The floor effect is complete. Under the fixed thresholds the rule detects no window of QQQ (0 of 3{,}867, windows ending 2011 to 2026-10-09) and none of IWM (0 of 4{,}678, 2008 to 2026-10-09), in any year and on either series. The largest 30-day mean $|\mathrm{GEX}|$ is \$4.92~billion for QQQ and \$4.98~billion for IWM on \ident{new_full} (\$4.11~billion and \$4.37~billion without same-day expiries), against \$16.00~billion for SPY, so no window reaches the \$5~billion criterion. This is a consequence of the fixed thresholds, a pre-registered design decision, and not a modelling result. The magnitude criterion is the binding one: the persistence and sign-flip tests alone pass 1{,}434 of 3{,}867 windows of QQQ (37.1\%) and 3{,}357 of 4{,}678 of IWM (71.8\%), against 1{,}763 of 4{,}671 windows of SPY (37.7\%). gpt-6-luna labels no window of QQQ or IWM as persistent either (0 of 3{,}867 and 0 of 4{,}678 on \ident{new_full}; 0 of 1{,}699 and 0 of 1{,}700 on \ident{new_ex0dte}), so the unscaled arms show that the model also rejects every window; they cannot test its discrimination.

% src: report.yaml diagnostics.scaling (factor, n_days_in_factor); series parquet new_full_scaled_QQQ / _IWM series_metadata (ratio_min, ratio_max, ratio_p25, ratio_p75); stats.yaml contrasts series_contrast family scale and period_shift for new_full_scaled
The scaled variant multiplies the \ident{new_full} values by one factor per symbol: 4.225 for QQQ (median of 1{,}699 daily ratios) and 11.431 for IWM (1{,}700 days). It is a disclosed protocol change (Section~\ref{sec:methods:arms}). With it the rule detects 350 of 1{,}670 windows of QQQ (21.0\%) and 901 of 1{,}671 windows of IWM (53.9\%), and gpt-6-luna 360 (21.6\%) and 904 (54.1\%). The model follows the rule on the scaled inputs: the verdicts agree in 99.4\% of the QQQ windows (kappa 0.982) and 99.7\% of the IWM windows (kappa 0.994). The paired difference to the unscaled arms is 21.0 percentage points for QQQ (quarter-block bootstrap interval [12.6, 31.2], $p=5.0\times10^{-4}$; exact McNemar test on the non-overlapping subsample $p=4.9\times10^{-4}$) and 53.9 for IWM ([40.7, 67.4], $p=5.0\times10^{-4}$; $p=4.7\times10^{-10}$); for the model the differences are 21.6 and 54.1 percentage points.

The scaled rates depend on the factor and are not a measurement of QQQ or IWM regimes. The ratio of the open-interest notional of SPY to that of the symbol is not constant: for QQQ the daily ratio runs from 0.78 to 38.32 (quartiles 3.30 and 5.73), and for IWM from 1.51 to 110.57 (quartiles 7.72 and 16.46). A single factor puts the exposure on the scale of SPY on a typical day, no more, and another factor would give other rates. The scaled arms therefore show only that the model follows the criteria once the floor is lifted. They show no step after 2023: the rate of the rule on the scaled IWM series is 65.2\% in 2020 to 2023 and 35.7\% in 2024 and 2025 (shift $-$29.5 percentage points, $p=0.061$ by bootstrap, $p=0.137$ by Fisher exact test on the subsample), and on the scaled QQQ series 25.9\% and 13.4\% ($-$12.5 percentage points, $p=0.170$, $p=0.292$).

\subsection{Post-Publication Period}
\label{sec:results:post}

\subsection{Model Fidelity to the Rule}
\label{sec:results:fidelity}

Table~\ref{tab:fidelity} and Figure~\ref{fig:fidelity} compare each model answer with the deterministic classifier on the same window.
% src: detection_by_year.csv label_source=llm scope=all (n_compared, agree_pct, kappa, metrics_agree_pct, sign_flips_exact_pct); disagreements.csv (58 rows; causes derived from llm_sign_flips, clf_sign_flips, llm_avg_magnitude_bn, clf_avg_magnitude_bn); figures/output/fig07_llm_classifier_fidelity.json extra.groups and extra.borderline
The verdict of the model equals the verdict of the rule in 99.6\% or more of the windows of every SPY arm (99.7\% on \ident{full_spy}, 99.7\% on \ident{ex0dte_spy}), with kappa between 0.985 and 0.995. The published o4-mini answers equal the rule in all 1{,}412 windows, and gpt-6-luna reaches 99.4\% and 99.7\% on the scaled QQQ and IWM series. On the unscaled QQQ and IWM arms the accuracy is 100.0\% and kappa is undefined, because the rule labels no window as persistent. Over the answers drawn in panels (c) to (e) of Figure~\ref{fig:fidelity} (33{,}160 parsed answers) the verdict equals the rule's in 99.8\% of the 31{,}684 gpt-6-luna answers and in 100.0\% of the 1{,}476 o4-mini answers.

The reported metrics are less exact than the verdict, and least exact on the unscaled QQQ and IWM arms (82.2\% to 89.5\%). The share of answers whose reported counts, magnitude and persistence equal the computed values (within 0.5 days, \$0.05 billion and 0.5 percentage points) is 99.4\% for o4-mini and between 92.7\% and 98.1\% for gpt-6-luna on the SPY arms. Its reported persistence lies within the tolerance of the figure (one day) in 94.9\% of the answers and its reported magnitude within 10\% of \$5 billion in 98.7\%, against 99.9\% and 99.6\% for o4-mini. The median ratio of reported to computed magnitude is 1.000.

The few verdicts that differ from the rule lie at the thresholds. Seven gpt-6-luna arms have windows in which the verdict differs from the rule (\ident{full_spy}, \ident{ex0dte_spy}, \ident{full_qqq_scaled}, \ident{bridge_old}, \ident{bridge_jan}, \ident{full_iwm_scaled}, \ident{p34_obf_2024}), 58 windows in all (\path{results/disagreements.csv}): 39 are windows in which the model reports at most five sign flips where the rule counts six or more, so that it calls a window persistent that the rule classifies as low conviction; 15 are windows in which the reported mean magnitude lies on the other side of \$5 billion than the computed one; 2 are overcounted flips; and 2 are windows whose reported values are correct and meet all three criteria but which the model labels transitional (persistence 70.0\% and 73.3\%, close to the 70\% threshold). In 41 of the 58 windows the model detects a regime that the rule rejects, and in 17 it rejects one that the rule detects. In 51 of the 58 windows the computed values lie in the borderline region of Figure~\ref{fig:fidelity}, that is a persistence within one day of 21 of 30 days (66.7--73.3\%), a mean magnitude between \$4.50 and \$5.50 billion or 4 to 6 flips. Away from these regions the model reproduces the rule.

\subsection{Dated versus Obfuscated Prompts: The 2024 and 2020 Baselines}
\label{sec:results:dated}

Table~\ref{tab:dated} compares the published o4-mini answers on the inputs of the Phase 3 and 4 baselines, whose prompts carried calendar dates, with gpt-6-luna on the same values with the dates and with relative day labels (Section~\ref{sec:methods:arms}).
% src: detection_by_year.csv scope=all (mean_abs_gex_bn of classifier p3_2024 and p4_2020; arms r1_p3_o4mini, r1_p4_o4mini, p34_dated_2024, p34_dated_2020, p34_obf_2024, p34_obf_2020; classifier p3_2024, p4_2020); stats.yaml rates (Wilson), contrasts series_contrast family label and phase_contrast; report.yaml headline.bridge; series parquet p3_2024_SPY, p4_2020_SPY
The published o4-mini answers detect 181 of 223 (81.2\%) of the 2024 windows and 27 of 223 (12.1\%) of the 2020 windows (Wilson 95\% intervals 75.5--85.8\% and 8.5--17.0\%). With the dates, gpt-6-luna reproduces these answers: it gives the same verdict in 223 of 223 windows of 2024 and 223 of 223 of 2020 (kappa 1.000 and 1.000). The deterministic classifier on the series as sent gives the same labels as o4-mini in both baselines (100.0\% and 100.0\% agreement).

Showing the dates changed nothing that the data can detect. With relative day labels gpt-6-luna detects 180 of 223 (80.7\%) of the 2024 windows against 181 of 223 (81.2\%) with dates, and 27 of 223 (12.1\%) of the 2020 windows against 27 of 223 (12.1\%). The two labelings differ in 1 window of 2024 and in none of 2020 (paired quarter-block bootstrap interval for 2024 [$-$1.9, 0.0] percentage points, $p=0.646$; exact McNemar test $p=1.000$ at stride one and $p=1.000$ on the non-overlapping subsample of 8 windows; in 2020 both are $p=1.000$). The 223 windows of each baseline overlap with stride one, and the quarter-block bootstrap rests on 4 calendar quarters, so these intervals are coarse.

The 2024-versus-2020 contrast of the earlier studies is reproduced under every label type. The difference in detection rate between the two baselines is 69.1 percentage points for the published o4-mini answers ($\varphi=0.692$, Newcombe--Wilson interval [61.6, 74.9]; quarter-block bootstrap interval [31.6, 93.3], $p=0.002$; Fisher exact test on the non-overlapping subsample of 8 windows per baseline $p=0.041$), 69.1 for gpt-6-luna with dates ($\varphi=0.692$) and 68.6 for gpt-6-luna with relative labels ($\varphi=0.688$; bootstrap $p=0.003$; Fisher $p=0.041$). The Fisher test on 8 non-overlapping windows per baseline is far less extreme than the test on all windows, which treats the overlapping windows as independent ($p=1.8\times10^{-52}$).

The contrast is one of magnitudes and not of regimes. The two input series are not on one convention: the 2024 values are a gross magnitude with an imposed negative sign and a mean absolute value of \$31.24~billion per day, the 2020 values are a magnitude without sign and have a mean of \$2.85~billion (Section~\ref{sec:results:inputs}). The 30-day mean $|\mathrm{GEX}|$ of the windows averages \$30.71~billion in 2024 and \$2.56~billion in 2020, against the criterion of \$5 billion, so the contrast does not describe a negative against a positive regime, and the type of day label does not change it.
