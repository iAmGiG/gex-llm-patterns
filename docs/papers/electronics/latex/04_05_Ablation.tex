\subsection{0DTE Ablation}
\label{sec:results:ablation}

Table~\ref{tab:ablation} separates the contribution of same-day expiries by comparing \ident{new_full} with \ident{new_ex0dte}.
% src: ex0dte_decomposition.csv (symbol SPY, QQQ, IWM; scope year; abs_0dte_share_pct, sign_flip_by_0dte_pct, rate_full_pct, rate_ex0dte_pct, n_full_only, n_ex0dte_only, llm_rate_*); stats.yaml contrasts series_contrast new_full versus new_ex0dte (paired by window end) for periods 2008-2019, 2020-2023, 2024, 2025, 2024-2025, 2026
The share of same-day expiries in the absolute net GEX of SPY grows from 0.9\% in 2008 and 11.5\% in 2013 to 22.1\% in 2019, 25.7\% in 2020 and 45.9\% in 2024, and it stands at 43.9\% in 2025 and 40.8\% in 2026 (the share is the sum of the absolute same-day-expiry contribution over the sum of the absolute net GEX of \ident{new_full} over the days of the year). For QQQ it is 45.5\% in 2025 and for IWM 23.0\%. The sign of the daily net GEX changes when same-day expiries are removed on 11.1\% of the days of 2024 and 13.2\% of the days of 2025 (4.4\% of all days).

The effect on the verdict is small and has no constant direction. In 2024 the rule detects 10.3\% of the windows of the complete series and 13.1\% of those without same-day expiries; 9 windows are detected only on the complete series and 16 only without same-day expiries (difference $-$2.8 percentage points; quarter-block bootstrap interval [$-$10.7, 5.5], $p=0.499$; exact McNemar test on the non-overlapping subsample $p=1.000$). The counts for 2025 are 4 and 11 (rates in Table~\ref{tab:ablation}). Pooled over 2024 and 2025 the rates are 12.0\% and 14.7\% for the rule (difference $-$2.8 percentage points, interval [$-$8.8, 2.8], $p=0.369$; McNemar on the subsample of 17 windows $p=1.000$) and 12.5\% and 15.3\% for gpt-6-luna ($-$2.8 percentage points, $p=0.376$; subsample $p=1.000$). Removing same-day expiries therefore does not restore the high rates of the published inputs: the rate is 14.7\% without them and 12.0\% with them, against 100.0\% on the published inputs. In 2020 to 2023 the complete series detects more than the ex-0DTE series (22.2\% against 18.1\%, 4.1 percentage points; $p=0.137$ by bootstrap, $p=1.000$ by McNemar on the subsample), and in 2026 it detects less (11.3\% against 21.1\%; 0 windows against 19 in the paired comparison; bootstrap $p=0.646$, McNemar on the subsample $p=1.000$). For QQQ and IWM the rule detects no window under either series, so the ablation has no effect on their verdicts.

The net GEX of SPY on 2024-03-15 is $+$\$14.61~billion on the published inputs and $+$\$14.61~billion as call-only GEX, $-$\$4.51~billion without same-day expiries and $-$\$15.35~billion on the complete series. The archive value is $+$\$12.62~billion and is put-less too, so the step from the archive to the complete series cannot be read as an effect of the gamma model alone: it combines the restored put leg with the change of the gamma model.
