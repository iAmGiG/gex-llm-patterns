\subsection{Open-Weights Models on Local Hardware}
\label{sec:results:open}

% src: llm_parse_stats.csv model=gpt-oss-20b (n_rows, n_parsed, n_fallback, n_failed, n_missing, n_rerun_at_retry_cap, mean_completion_tokens); cost_ledger.csv model=gpt-oss-20b (n_requests, summed); runs parquet run_metadata.local.seconds (summed over the local runs); llm_parse_stats.csv gpt-6-luna arms bridge_jan, full_spy, ex0dte_spy (mean_completion_tokens 736 to 788)
The seven open-weights arms repeat the bridge, the two complete SPY arms and the four baseline arms with gpt-oss-20b (Section~\ref{sec:methods:model}). They sent 12{,}398 requests, the 40-window pilot, the 5\% repeats and the retries included, in 10.0 GPU-hours on the shared A100 GPUs, at no cost. Fourteen first-pass answers ended at the limit of 4{,}000 completion tokens; each was requested again at 8{,}000 tokens, and every retry parsed. Of the 11{,}750 windows of the seven arms, 11{,}748 hold a parsed answer. The two that do not are windows of \ident{oss_full_spy} whose answer closes the reasoning field with a typographic quotation mark followed by control tokens of the model, so that no parser recovers the JSON object; one answer of \ident{oss_bridge_jan} with the same defect was recovered by the fallback parser. The answers of gpt-oss-20b are longer: between 1{,}126 and 1{,}995 completion tokens per arm on average, against 736 to 788 for gpt-6-luna on the bridge and the complete series.

% src: bridge_agreement.csv comparison=bridge ref_arm=r1_o4mini other_arm=oss_bridge_jan scope=all (n_pairs 1412; 646, 0, 6, 760; agree_pct 99.575071; kappa 0.991446; p_mcnemar_exact 0.03125); the same row for other_arm=bridge_jan (646, 0, 5, 761; kappa 0.992871); stats.yaml rates llm oss_bridge_jan 2024-2025 (501 of 501); stats.yaml period_shift oss_bridge_jan (diff_pp 82.359, bootstrap p 0.0005)
On the published inputs gpt-oss-20b gives the verdict of o4-mini in 1{,}406 of the 1{,}412 published windows (99.6\%, kappa 0.991), as gpt-6-luna does in 1{,}407 (99.6\%, kappa 0.993). Like gpt-6-luna it detects every window that o4-mini detects, and it adds 6 detections where gpt-6-luna adds 5 (exact McNemar test at stride one $p=0.031$). It detects all 501 windows of 2024 and 2025 on these inputs, so the open-weights model reproduces the published saturation from the published inputs, with a rise of 82.4 percentage points from 2020--2023 to 2024--2025 (quarter-block bootstrap $p=0.0005$).

% src: detection_by_year.csv label_source=llm scope=all arm=oss_full_spy (n_compared 4689, agree_pct 99.722755, kappa 0.987793) and arm=full_spy (99.658921, 0.984988); arm=oss_ex0dte_spy and ex0dte_spy; stats.yaml rates llm oss_full_spy 2024-2025 (62 of 502, bootstrap 5.675-19.798), classifier new_full 2024-2025 (60 of 502), llm full_spy 2024-2025 (63 of 502); stats.yaml period_shift oss_full_spy (diff_pp -10.137, p_boot 0.103) and classifier new_full (-10.2, 0.108); stats.yaml rates llm oss_ex0dte_spy 2024-2025 (75 of 502), classifier new_ex0dte (74 of 502), llm ex0dte_spy (77 of 502)
On the complete series gpt-oss-20b follows the rule as closely as gpt-6-luna does. It agrees with the rule in 99.7\% of the 4{,}689 parsed windows of \ident{oss_full_spy} (kappa 0.988, against 0.985 for gpt-6-luna) and in 99.9\% of the 4{,}691 windows of \ident{oss_ex0dte_spy} (kappa 0.992, against 0.984). For 2024 and 2025 it detects 62 of 502 windows (12.4\%; quarter-block bootstrap 5.7--19.8\%), between the rule (60, 12.0\%) and gpt-6-luna (63, 12.5\%), and its change from 2020--2023 to 2024--2025 is $-$10.1 percentage points (bootstrap $p=0.103$), against $-$10.2 for the rule ($p=0.108$). Without same-day expiries it detects 75 of 502 windows of 2024 and 2025 (14.9\%), against 14.7\% for the rule and 15.3\% for gpt-6-luna, so for this model, too, removing them does not restore the published rates.

The two current models give the same verdict in 99.4\% of the windows of the complete series (4{,}660 of 4{,}689; kappa 0.973) and in 99.6\% without same-day expiries (4{,}670 of 4{,}691; kappa 0.976). Where they differ, the rule sides with gpt-oss-20b in 16 and with gpt-6-luna in 13 of the 29 windows of \ident{new_full}, and in 14 and 7 of the 21 windows of \ident{new_ex0dte}, so gpt-oss-20b follows the criteria at least as closely as gpt-6-luna. On the windows that both answered, the two models detect almost the same number (gpt-6-luna 616 and gpt-oss-20b 617 on \ident{new_full}, 495 and 496 on \ident{new_ex0dte}; exact McNemar test $p=1.000$ in both). On the published inputs they agree in 99.2\% of the 1{,}476 windows (kappa 0.984), with 6 of the 12 differences resolved in favour of each.

For gpt-oss-20b as for gpt-6-luna, the dates in the prompt changed nothing that the data can detect. With relative labels it detects 185 of 223 windows of the 2024 baseline (83.0\%) and with the dates 183 of 223 (82.1\%); the two labelings differ in 6 windows, 4 detected with relative labels only and 2 with dates only (paired quarter-block bootstrap interval [0.0, 2.4] percentage points, $p=0.633$; exact McNemar test $p=0.688$ at stride one and $p=1.000$ on the non-overlapping subsample of 8 windows), and in 2020 both labelings detect 27 of 223. With the dates it differs from the published o4-mini answers in 2 windows of 2024, both detected by gpt-oss-20b only, and in none of 2020. The 2024-versus-2020 contrast is 70.9 percentage points with relative labels ($\varphi=0.709$) and 70.0 with dates ($\varphi=0.701$), with the Fisher test on the non-overlapping subsample at $p=0.041$ in both cases. The 2024 baseline with relative labels is the arm where gpt-oss-20b departs most from the rule (185 detections against 181; agreement 98.2\%, kappa 0.939).

Repeated requests give the first verdict again in 74 of 74 bridge windows, 234 of 235 windows of the complete series, 241 of 241 windows without same-day expiries and 11 of 11 in each baseline arm. The reported metrics match the computed ones about as well as those of gpt-6-luna: on the complete series the reported mean $|\mathrm{GEX}|$ lies within 5\% of the computed value in 99.5\% of the answers (gpt-6-luna 98.7\%), and the sign-flip count is exact in 93.4\% (gpt-6-luna 93.4\%). A model whose weights are fixed at one revision therefore reproduces the bridge, the rates of the complete series and the absence of a date effect, so these results do not depend on the hosted alias.

% src: llm_parse_stats.csv model=llama-3.1-8b-instruct (n_rows, n_parsed = n_ok, n_finish_length 0, mean_completion_tokens 167 to 171); cost_ledger.csv model=llama-3.1-8b-instruct (n_requests summed 12,378); runs parquet run_metadata.local.seconds (summed 11,904 s = 3.31 h); detection_by_year.csv label_source=llm scope=all arm=llama_* (rate_pct 100.0 or 99.978683, agree_pct, kappa 0.000 to 0.000064); detections parquet llama_full_spy and llama_ex0dte_spy (the one regime_detected = false answer of each: window_end 2010-01-28); bridge_agreement.csv comparison=test_retest ref_arm=llama_* (74 + 235 + 235 + 4 x 11 = 588 pairs, agree_pct 100.0); bridge_agreement.csv comparison=bridge ref_arm=r1_p4_o4mini other_arm=llama_p34_dated_2020 (27, 0, 196, 0); stats.yaml rates llm llama_bridge_jan 2024-2025 (501 of 501), phase_contrast llama_p34_dated_2024 vs llama_p34_dated_2020 (diff_pp 0.0)
Llama~3.1~8B answers the same seven arms with 12{,}378 requests, the pilot and the 5\% repeats included, in 3.3 GPU-hours. Every answer parses at the first attempt and none comes near the token limit (about 170 completion tokens on average), but the verdicts carry no information about the window: the model detects a regime in all 1{,}476 windows of the bridge, in all 223 windows of each baseline arm and in 4{,}690 of the 4{,}691 windows of each complete series, the exception being the same window (ending 2010-01-28) in both. Its agreement with the rule therefore equals the detection rate of the rule (45.2\% on the bridge, 13.0\% and 10.5\% on the complete series, 81.2\% and 12.1\% on the baselines), with kappa 0.000 in every arm, and repeated requests return the first verdict in all 588 repeated windows. On the published inputs it detects all 501 windows of 2024 and 2025, as o4-mini did, but it also detects all 223 windows of the 2020 baseline, where o4-mini detected 27, so its 2024-versus-2020 contrast is zero. A detection rate of 100\% in 2024 and 2025 is thus what a constant answer gives; the contrast with 2020 is the part of the published test that a model has to pass, and Llama~3.1~8B does not pass it.

% src: reported_vs_computed.csv basis=arm scope=all (ratio_within_5pct_pct llama_* 14.349776 to 64.125561; gpt-6-luna arms 98.443829 to 100.0); detection_by_year.csv label_source=llm scope=all (sign_flips_exact_pct llama_* 2.690583 to 13.901345); detections parquet llama_full_spy_..._20261108T175252Z_c0 custom_id llama_full_spy|SPY|2008-02-27 (regime_detected true, persistent_negative; reported 10 positive and 20 negative days, 66.67% persistence, 2.45 bn, 4 flips; reasoning quoted); planning/02_decisions_log.md D-30 (exploratory reorder run, kept in data/backups/gex_llm/exploratory_llama_reorder_20261108: 40 of 40 parsed, 32 detected, 26 equal to the rule)
The metrics that Llama~3.1~8B reports do not follow the inputs either. Its reported mean $|\mathrm{GEX}|$ lies within 5\% of the computed value in 14.3\% to 64.1\% of the answers per arm (gpt-6-luna: at least 98.4\%), and its sign-flip count is exact in 2.7\% to 13.9\%. The schema puts the verdict first, so this model, which has no reasoning phase, writes the verdict before any working, and the reasoning field then argues for the verdict with comparisons that are false: for the window of \ident{new_full} ending 2008-02-27 it writes that ``the persistence percentage of 66.67\% meets the 70\% threshold'' and that ``the average magnitude of \$2.45B is greater than the \$5B threshold''. The order of the fields is not the whole explanation: in an exploratory run outside the stored arms, with the reasoning and the counts placed before the verdict, the model still detected 32 of the 40 pilot windows and agreed with the rule in 26. At this size a model without a reasoning phase does not perform the task, so this arm cannot test whether a model of a second developer follows the rule.
