\subsection{Open-Weights Models on Local Hardware}
\label{sec:results:open}

% src: Section 3.5 (three open-weights models, D-29, D-30, D-32); Supplementary Materials Section S3 (Tables S6--S8) and Tables S3--S5 (local runs)
Three open-weights models, each from a different developer, answer the seven arms that repeat the bridge, the two complete SPY arms and the four baseline arms (Section~\ref{sec:methods:model}): gpt-oss-20b of OpenAI and Qwen3-8B of Alibaba, which reason before they answer, and Llama~3.1~8B of Meta, which does not. They ran on the shared A100 GPUs at no cost. Table~\ref{tab:open} compares their verdicts with the rule arm by arm; Section~S3 of the Supplementary Materials gives the detail per model (Tables~S6--S8) and Tables~S3--S5 list the runs.

% src: cost_ledger.csv model=gpt-oss-20b (n_requests summed 12,398); runs parquet run_metadata.local.seconds (summed over the oss runs, 10.0 h); llm_parse_stats.csv model=gpt-oss-20b arm_role=primary (n_rows 11,750, n_parsed 11,748; n_rerun_at_retry_cap 14 over all oss arms); bridge_agreement.csv comparison=bridge ref_arm=r1_o4mini scope=all other_arm=oss_bridge_jan (n_pairs 1412; 646, 0, 6, 760; agree_pct 99.575071; kappa 0.991446) and other_arm=bridge_jan (646, 0, 5, 761; kappa 0.992871); stats.yaml rates llm oss_bridge_jan 2024-2025 (501 of 501); detection_by_year.csv label_source=llm scope=all arm=oss_full_spy (agree_pct 99.722755, kappa 0.987793) and arm=full_spy (kappa 0.984988); stats.yaml rates llm oss_full_spy 2024-2025 (62 of 502, bootstrap 5.675-19.798), classifier new_full (60 of 502), llm full_spy (63 of 502); bridge_agreement.csv comparison=model_pair ref_arm=full_spy other_arm=oss_full_spy scope=all (agree_pct 99.381531, kappa 0.973; n_disagree_with_clf 29: 16 other_matches_clf, 13 ref_matches_clf); stats.yaml contrasts series_contrast family=label oss_p34_obf_2024 vs oss_p34_dated_2024 (185 and 183 of 223; naive_stride1 p_mcnemar_exact 0.6875)
The arms of gpt-oss-20b sent 12{,}398 requests, the 40-window pilot, the 5\% repeats and 14 retries at 8{,}000 tokens included, in 10.0 GPU-hours, and 11{,}748 of the 11{,}750 windows of its seven arms hold a parsed answer. On the published inputs it gives the verdict of o4-mini in 1{,}406 of the 1{,}412 published windows (99.6\%, kappa 0.991), as gpt-6-luna does in 1{,}407 (kappa 0.993), and it detects all 501 windows of 2024 and 2025, so it reproduces the published saturation from the published inputs. On the complete series it agrees with the rule in 99.7\% of the windows (kappa 0.988, against 0.985 for gpt-6-luna) and detects 62 of the 502 windows of 2024 and 2025 (12.4\%; quarter-block bootstrap 5.7--19.8\%), between the rule (60) and gpt-6-luna (63). It gives the verdict of gpt-6-luna in 99.4\% of the windows of the complete series (kappa 0.973), and where the two differ the rule sides with gpt-oss-20b in 16 and with gpt-6-luna in 13 of 29 windows. In the 2024 baseline it detects 185 of 223 windows with relative labels and 183 with dates (exact McNemar test $p=0.688$), so it shows no date effect either. A model whose weights are fixed at one revision therefore reproduces the bridge, the rates of the complete series and the absence of a date effect, and these results do not depend on the hosted alias.

% src: cost_ledger.csv model=llama-3.1-8b-instruct (n_requests summed 12,378); runs parquet run_metadata.local.seconds (summed 11,904 s = 3.31 h); llm_parse_stats.csv model=llama-3.1-8b-instruct (n_parsed = n_rows); detection_by_year.csv label_source=llm scope=all arm=llama_* (n_detect, rate_pct 100.0 or 99.978683, agree_pct, kappa 0.000 to 0.000064); bridge_agreement.csv comparison=bridge ref_arm=r1_p4_o4mini other_arm=llama_p34_dated_2020 (27, 0, 196, 0); stats.yaml rates llm llama_bridge_jan 2024-2025 (501 of 501), phase_contrast llama_p34_dated_2024 vs llama_p34_dated_2020 (diff_pp 0.0); reported_vs_computed.csv basis=arm scope=all (ratio_within_5pct_pct llama_* 14.349776 to 64.125561; gpt-6-luna arms 98.443829 to 100.0); detections parquet llama_full_spy_..._20261108T175252Z_c0 custom_id llama_full_spy|SPY|2008-02-27 (reported 10 positive and 20 negative days, 66.67% persistence; reasoning quoted)
Llama~3.1~8B answers the same seven arms with 12{,}378 requests in 3.3 GPU-hours, and every answer parses, but its verdicts carry no information about the window: it detects a regime in all 1{,}476 windows of the bridge, in all 223 windows of each baseline arm and in 4{,}690 of the 4{,}691 windows of each complete series. Its agreement with the rule therefore equals the detection rate of the rule, with kappa 0.000 in every arm (Table~\ref{tab:open}). On the published inputs it detects all 501 windows of 2024 and 2025, as o4-mini did, but it also detects all 223 windows of the 2020 baseline, where o4-mini detected 27, so its 2024-versus-2020 contrast is zero. A detection rate of 100\% in 2024 and 2025 is what a constant answer gives; the contrast with 2020 is the part of the published test that a model has to pass, and Llama~3.1~8B does not pass it. The metrics it reports do not follow the inputs either: its mean $|\mathrm{GEX}|$ lies within 5\% of the computed value in 14.3\% to 64.1\% of the answers per arm, against at least 98.4\% for gpt-6-luna. The schema puts the verdict first, so this model, which has no reasoning phase, writes its verdict before any working and then argues for it with comparisons that are false; for a window of \ident{new_full} whose 30 days are all negative it reports 10 positive and 20 negative days and writes that ``the persistence percentage of 66.67\% meets the 70\% threshold''. Under this verdict-first schema Llama~3.1~8B does not perform the task; a schema with the reasoning field first was not tested.

% src: llm_parse_stats.csv model=qwen3-8b arm_role=primary (mean_completion_tokens 3,728 to 5,657; n_rows 11,750; n_parsed 10,718; n_finish_length = n_rows - n_parsed in every arm); llm_parse_stats.csv gpt-6-luna arms (mean_completion_tokens 736 to 788); cost_ledger.csv model=qwen3-8b (n_requests summed 22,400; first pass 12,378; the retry runs, run_metadata.retry_of, 10,022); runs parquet run_metadata.local.seconds (summed 354,438 s = 98.5 h); missing_answer_bounds.csv period=all arm=qwen_* (n_unparsed summed 1,032, n_unparsed_clf_no_detect summed 1,011; qwen_full_spy parsed_pct_clf_detect 98.850575, parsed_pct_clf_no_detect 88.290054); planning/02_decisions_log.md D-32 (bounds)
Qwen3-8B reasons at much greater length: its answers average 3{,}728 to 5{,}657 completion tokens per arm, against 558 to 788 for gpt-6-luna on the same arms. Of its 12{,}378 first-pass requests, 10{,}022 reached the limit of 4{,}000 tokens and were requested again at 8{,}000, so its arms sent 22{,}400 requests in 98.5 GPU-hours. After the retry, 10{,}718 of the 11{,}750 windows of the seven arms (91.2\%) hold a parsed answer; each of the other 1{,}032 reached 8{,}000 tokens before the JSON object. The unanswered windows are not a random subset: 1{,}011 of them are windows that the rule rejects, so on the complete series Qwen3-8B answers 98.9\% of the windows that the rule detects but 88.3\% of those that it rejects (Table~S8). A rate over the answered windows would therefore overstate the detection rate, and we report the rate as bounds: the lower bound counts every unanswered window as not detected, the upper bound counts it as detected.

% src: detection_by_year.csv label_source=llm scope=all arm=qwen_full_spy (n_compared 4206, agree_pct 98.692344, kappa 0.948367), arm=qwen_ex0dte_spy (99.444058, 0.972522); bridge_agreement.csv comparison=model_pair ref_arm=full_spy other_arm=qwen_full_spy scope=all (agree_pct 98.50214, kappa 0.941087); bridge_agreement.csv comparison=bridge ref_arm=r1_o4mini other_arm=qwen_bridge_jan scope=all (1,287 pairs; 643, 2, 12, 630; agree_pct 98.912199; kappa 0.978243); detection_by_year.csv qwen_bridge_jan year 2024 and 2025 (251 and 250 parsed, all detected); disagreements.csv arm=qwen_* arm_role=primary (108 rows, 99 with llm_detected true and clf_detected false; 83 of these with clf_sign_flips 6 to 10 and the other criteria met, llm_sign_flips 3 to 5); disagreements.csv arm=full_spy (12 rows with llm_detected true and clf_detected false, all with clf_sign_flips > 5 and the other criteria met)
Where it answers, Qwen3-8B follows the rule: it agrees with the rule in 98.7\% of the answered windows of the complete series (kappa 0.948) and in 99.4\% without same-day expiries (kappa 0.973), and with gpt-6-luna in 98.5\% (kappa 0.941). On the published inputs it gives the verdict of o4-mini in 1{,}273 of the 1{,}287 published windows that it answered (98.9\%, kappa 0.978), and it answers and detects all 501 windows of 2024 and 2025. Its departures from the rule lean one way: in 99 of the 108 windows of the seven arms where it differs, it detects a regime that the rule rejects, and in 83 of these the only criterion that the window fails is the limit of five sign flips. The window has six to ten flips, and Qwen3-8B reports three to five. gpt-6-luna makes the same error less often (in 24 windows of the same seven arms).

% src: missing_answer_bounds.csv period=2024-2025 arm=qwen_full_spy (n_windows 502, n_parsed 399, n_detected_parsed 73, lower_pct 14.541833, upper_pct 35.059761, clf_rate_pct 11.952191) and arm=qwen_ex0dte_spy (465 parsed, 80 detected, 15.936255 to 23.306773, clf 14.741036); detection_by_year.csv label_source=llm arm=qwen_full_spy scope=year 2024 (n_parsed 190, n_detect 32, clf_rate_matched_pct 12.105263: 23 windows) and 2025 (209, 41, 16.267943: 34 windows); stats.yaml rates llm full_spy 2024-2025 (63 of 502); stats.yaml contrasts series_contrast family=label qwen_p34_obf_2024 vs qwen_p34_dated_2024 (n_common_windows 190, rates 93.684211 and 95.789474, windows_only 1 and 5, bootstrap_quarter p 0.125); missing_answer_bounds.csv period=all (qwen_p34_obf_2024 lower 82.06278, qwen_p34_dated_2024 lower 83.856502, parsed_pct_clf_no_detect 78.571429 and 45.238095, qwen_p34_dated_2020 upper 14.349776); llm_parse_stats.csv qwen_p34_obf_2020 (223 of 223 parsed; rate 12.107623); missing_answer_bounds.csv n_unparsed_clf_no_detect qwen_p34_dated_2024 23 and qwen_p34_obf_2024 9 of the 42 rule-rejected windows (19 and 33 answered); disagreements.csv arm=qwen_p34_dated_2024 (9 rows, all llm_detected true, clf_detected false); detection_by_year.csv arm=qwen_p34_dated_2024 scope=all (kappa 0.667542)
The same error raises its rate in 2024 and 2025. On the 399 windows of the complete series that it answered it detects 73 and the rule 57, and over all 502 windows, 103 of them unanswered, its rate lies between 14.5\% and 35.1\%, against 12.0\% for the rule and 12.5\% for gpt-6-luna; without same-day expiries the bounds are 15.9\% and 23.3\%, against 14.7\% for the rule. Even the upper bound is far below the published 100\%. In the 2024 baseline the dates change nothing that the data can detect: on the 190 windows answered under both labelings it detects 178 with relative labels and 182 with dates (quarter-block bootstrap $p=0.125$). With dates, however, it answers fewer of the windows that the rule rejects (19 of 42, against 33 with relative labels) and detects 9 of the 19, which makes this arm its largest departure from the rule (kappa 0.668). Its 2024-versus-2020 contrast is at least 70.0 percentage points with relative labels and 69.5 with dates (lower bound for 2024 minus upper bound for 2020), so it passes the part of the test that Llama~3.1~8B fails.

% src: the three paragraphs above
Two reasoning models of two developers thus read the windows much as the rule does wherever they answer (kappa 0.67 to 1.00 for Qwen3-8B), reproduce the bridge and the 2024-versus-2020 contrast, and find no saturation on the complete series, while the one model without a reasoning phase gives a constant answer. On the windows that Qwen3-8B answers, the agreement with the rule is therefore not specific to the developer of gpt-6-luna.
