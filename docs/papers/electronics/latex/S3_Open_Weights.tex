\section{Open-Weights Models: Detail}
\label{sec:s:open}

Section~4.12 of the article compares the three open-weights models in one table. This section gives the detail per model: the rates of gpt-oss-20b next to the rule and gpt-6-luna (Table~\ref{tab:s:oss}), the verdicts and the reported metrics of Llama~3.1~8B (Table~\ref{tab:s:llama}), and the answered windows of Qwen3-8B by the verdict of the rule, with the bounds of its detection rate (Table~\ref{tab:s:qwen}). The runs are listed in Tables~\ref{tab:ledger3}--\ref{tab:ledger5}. Rates and agreements are over the first pass of each arm; the repeats enter only the test-retest counts.

\subsection{gpt-oss-20b}
\label{sec:s:open:oss}

% src: llm_parse_stats.csv model=gpt-oss-20b (n_rows, n_parsed, n_fallback, n_failed, n_missing, n_rerun_at_retry_cap, mean_completion_tokens); llm_parse_stats.csv gpt-6-luna arms bridge_jan, full_spy, ex0dte_spy (mean_completion_tokens 736 to 788); bridge_agreement.csv comparison=bridge ref_arm=r1_o4mini other_arm=oss_bridge_jan scope=all (646, 0, 6, 760; p_mcnemar_exact 0.03125); stats.yaml period_shift oss_bridge_jan (diff_pp 82.359, bootstrap p 0.0005), oss_full_spy (diff_pp -10.137, p_boot 0.103) and classifier new_full (-10.2, 0.108); stats.yaml rates llm oss_ex0dte_spy 2024-2025 (75 of 502), classifier new_ex0dte (74 of 502), llm ex0dte_spy (77 of 502)
Fourteen first-pass answers of gpt-oss-20b ended at the limit of 4{,}000 completion tokens; each was requested again at 8{,}000 tokens, and every retry parsed. The two windows without a parsed answer are windows of \ident{oss_full_spy} whose answer closes the reasoning field with a typographic quotation mark followed by control tokens of the model, so that no parser recovers the JSON object; one answer of \ident{oss_bridge_jan} with the same defect was recovered by the fallback parser. The answers are longer than those of gpt-6-luna: between 1{,}126 and 1{,}995 completion tokens per arm on average, against 736 to 788 on the bridge and the complete series. On the published inputs gpt-oss-20b detects every window that o4-mini detects and adds 6 detections where gpt-6-luna adds 5 (exact McNemar test at stride one $p=0.031$); its rise from 2020--2023 to 2024--2025 is 82.4 percentage points (quarter-block bootstrap $p=0.0005$). On the complete series its change between the same periods is $-$10.1 percentage points (bootstrap $p=0.103$), against $-$10.2 for the rule ($p=0.108$). Without same-day expiries it detects 75 of the 502 windows of 2024 and 2025 (14.9\%), against 14.7\% for the rule and 15.3\% for gpt-6-luna, so for this model, too, removing them does not restore the published rates.

Where they differ, the rule sides with gpt-oss-20b in 16 and with gpt-6-luna in 13 of the 29 windows of \ident{new_full}, and in 14 and 7 of the 21 windows of \ident{new_ex0dte}. On the windows that both answered, the two models detect almost the same number (gpt-6-luna 616 and gpt-oss-20b 617 on \ident{new_full}, 495 and 496 on \ident{new_ex0dte}; exact McNemar test $p=1.000$ in both). On the published inputs they agree in 99.2\% of the 1{,}476 windows (kappa 0.984), with 6 of the 12 differences resolved in favour of each.

With relative labels gpt-oss-20b detects 185 of the 223 windows of the 2024 baseline (83.0\%) and with the dates 183 (82.1\%); the two labelings differ in 6 windows, 4 detected with relative labels only and 2 with dates only (paired quarter-block bootstrap interval [0.0, 2.4] percentage points, $p=0.633$; exact McNemar test $p=0.688$ at stride one and $p=1.000$ on the non-overlapping subsample of 8 windows), and in 2020 both labelings detect 27 of 223. With the dates it differs from the published o4-mini answers in 2 windows of 2024, both detected by gpt-oss-20b only, and in none of 2020. The 2024-versus-2020 contrast is 70.9 percentage points with relative labels ($\varphi=0.709$) and 70.0 with dates ($\varphi=0.701$), with the Fisher test on the non-overlapping subsample at $p=0.041$ in both cases. The 2024 baseline with relative labels is the arm where gpt-oss-20b departs most from the rule (185 detections against 181; agreement 98.2\%, kappa 0.939).

\subsection{Llama~3.1~8B}
\label{sec:s:open:llama}

% src: llm_parse_stats.csv model=llama-3.1-8b-instruct (n_rows, n_parsed = n_ok, n_finish_length 0, mean_completion_tokens 167 to 171); detections parquet llama_full_spy and llama_ex0dte_spy (the one regime_detected = false answer of each: window_end 2010-01-28); bridge_agreement.csv comparison=test_retest ref_arm=llama_* (74 + 235 + 235 + 4 x 11 = 588 pairs, agree_pct 100.0); detection_by_year.csv label_source=llm scope=all arm=llama_* (agree_pct, kappa, sign_flips_exact_pct 2.690583 to 13.901345); reported_vs_computed.csv basis=arm scope=all (ratio_within_5pct_pct llama_* 14.349776 to 64.125561); detections parquet llama_full_spy_..._20261108T175252Z_c0 custom_id llama_full_spy|SPY|2008-02-27 (reported 10 positive and 20 negative days, 66.67% persistence, 2.45 bn, 4 flips; reasoning quoted)
Every answer of Llama~3.1~8B parses at the first attempt and none comes near the token limit (about 170 completion tokens on average). The one window that it does not detect in each complete series is the same window, ending 2010-01-28. Because it detects nearly every window, its agreement with the rule equals the detection rate of the rule (45.2\% on the bridge, 13.0\% and 10.5\% on the complete series, 81.2\% and 12.1\% on the baselines), with kappa 0.000 in every arm, and repeated requests return the first verdict in all 588 repeated windows. Its reported mean $|\mathrm{GEX}|$ lies within 5\% of the computed value in 14.3\% to 64.1\% of the answers per arm and its sign-flip count is exact in 2.7\% to 13.9\% (Table~\ref{tab:s:llama}). For the window of \ident{new_full} ending 2008-02-27 it reports 10 positive and 20 negative days and writes that ``the persistence percentage of 66.67\% meets the 70\% threshold'' and that ``the average magnitude of \$2.45B is greater than the \$5B threshold''.

\label{sec:s:open:qwen}

Table~\ref{tab:s:qwen} gives the share of windows that Qwen3-8B answered in each arm, split by the verdict of the rule, its agreement on the answered windows and the bounds of its detection rate. On the 2020 baselines it answers 223 and 218 of the 223 windows; on the 2024 baseline it answers 33 of the 42 windows that the rule rejects with relative labels and 19 of them with dates. On the published inputs it answers 1{,}287 of the 1{,}412 published windows and differs from o4-mini in 14 (12 detected by Qwen3-8B only), and in all 14 the rule sides with o4-mini (exact McNemar test at stride one $p=0.013$). With the dates it differs from the published o4-mini answers in 9 of the 197 windows of 2024 that it answered, all detected by Qwen3-8B only ($p=0.004$), and in 1 of the 218 of 2020.

Of the 99 windows that Qwen3-8B detects and the rule rejects, 83 fail only the limit of five sign flips: they have six to ten flips (52 have six), and Qwen3-8B reports three to five. Of the others, 14 fail only the magnitude criterion (9 of them with a computed mean $|\mathrm{GEX}|$ between \$4.96 and \$5.00 billion, which Qwen3-8B reports as \$4.97 to \$5.00 billion and accepts), 1 fails only the persistence criterion and 1 fails both the magnitude criterion and the flip limit. In the 9 windows that the rule detects and Qwen3-8B does not, it reports a mean $|\mathrm{GEX}|$ below \$5 billion where the computed mean is above it in 7, a lower persistence in 1 and more sign flips in 1. Its reported mean $|\mathrm{GEX}|$ lies within 5\% of the computed value in 96.9\% to 99.5\% of the answered windows per arm, and its sign-flip count is exact in 86.3\% to 100.0\% (87.3\% on the complete series, against 93.4\% for gpt-6-luna). Repeated requests give the first verdict again in 57 of 57 bridge windows, 193 of 195 windows of the complete series, 196 of 197 without same-day expiries and 34 of 34 in the baselines, counting the windows answered in both passes.
