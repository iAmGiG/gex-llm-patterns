\subsection{Regime Criteria}
\label{sec:methods:criteria}

A 30-trading-day window is a persistent regime when three criteria hold \cite{regan2026regimes,regan2026jrfm}: (i)~persistence, at least 21 of the 30 days (70\%) share the sign of net GEX; (ii)~magnitude, the mean absolute net GEX is at least \$5~billion; and (iii)~stability, the sign changes at most five times. The deterministic classifier (the rule) labels every other window as low conviction (a persistent sign as in (i) and a mean magnitude of at least \$3~billion, but failing (ii) or (iii)) or transitional. The detection label used throughout is the binary persistent or not-persistent verdict (\ident{regime_detected} for the model, \ident{clf_should_detect} for the rule). The thresholds are those of the earlier studies and are not re-tuned.
% src: src/validation/regime_classifier.py (positive days are values > 0; classification order); report.yaml diagnostics.thresholds
Zero GEX counts as non-positive. A window with a persistent sign and a mean magnitude of at least \$5~billion but more than five sign changes fails the stability criterion and is labelled low conviction, because its mean still exceeds \$3~billion. The prompt shows values rounded to 0.01B, whereas the rule replays the unrounded USD values stored with each window. On 4 of the 38{,}592 windows the rounded values would give another four-way label, and on two of them (scaled QQQ, window ends 2021-03-05 and 2021-03-08) another verdict. The thresholds are fixed in dollars before the runs, for every symbol. This choice has a consequence for QQQ and IWM, whose gamma is smaller than that of SPY, and Section~S3 of the Supplementary Materials reports the resulting floor effect. The label that these criteria define is a GEX label: it is not a measured dealer position and not a demonstrated price regime.

\subsection{Daily GEX Series}
\label{sec:methods:series}

For each symbol and trading day $t$ the net dealer gamma exposure is
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{GEX}_t=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{C}_t} s_i\,\Gamma_i\,\mathrm{OI}_i\cdot 100\cdot S_t^{2}\cdot 0.01,
\qquad s_i=\begin{cases}+1 & \text{call,}\\ -1 & \text{put,}\end{cases}
\label{eq:gex}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{C}_t$ is the set of contracts listed on day $t$ with a defined gamma $\Gamma_i$, positive open interest $\mathrm{OI}_i$ and a positive underlying price $S_t$. The series of Table~\ref{tab:series} differ in how $\Gamma_i$ is obtained, in which expiries and legs enter $\mathcal{C}_t$, and in where the values come from.

\subsection{Windows and Deterministic Classifier}
\label{sec:methods:windows}

A window consists of 30 consecutive rows of a series (days $T{-}29$ to $T{+}0$), with one window per end date (stride one) and end dates up to 2026-10-09. Three detectors flag a window whose span covers a calendar gap of more than four days, a trading day of the symbol's master calendar that the series lacks, or a NYSE trading day that the series lacks (\ident{nyse_missing_days}). A flag marks a window but does not drop it, and flagged windows are kept in every rate. The detectors, and Table~S12 of the Supplementary Materials, which gives the 38{,}592 windows by series and flag, are described in Section~S8.
% src: condensed/windows.csv.gz series_key=new_full (window_end of the windows with nyse_missing_days > 0: 2018-11, 2018-12, 2019-09, 2019-10, 2020-06 to 2020-08 for SPY, plus 2024-10 and 2024-11 for QQQ); series parquet new_full_SPY (calendar gaps above four days: 2012-10-26 to 2012-10-31, 2019-08-30 to 2019-09-04)
Values are relabelled ``Day T-29'' to ``Day T+0'' with no dates or tickers, exactly as in Phase 4A, except in the two dated arms of Section~\ref{sec:methods:arms}, which carry the ISO dates of the November 2025 prompts. The rule of Section~\ref{sec:methods:criteria} labels every window independently of any model call, and its verdict is the reference label for each arm. The windows that end in 2026, up to 2026-10-09, form the post-publication period (Section~\ref{sec:results:post}): the published sample ends on 2025-12-31, so the 29 earliest of these windows still contain days of it, and those with ends from 2026-02-13 lie entirely after it.
% src: condensed/windows.csv.gz series_key=new_full symbol=SPY window_end >= 2026-01-01 (194 windows, 29 end before 2026-02-13)
The January inputs and the four Phase 3 and 4 inputs keep their own calendars (rule J), and a build stops unless it reproduces the surviving batch file byte for byte. Of the 1{,}476 January windows, 58 span one of the two dates that the January calendar lacks (the master-calendar flag) and 87 span a NYSE trading day that the series lacks (these 58 and the 29 windows that span 2020-06-23); all 87 are flagged and kept (Table~S12). Windows overlap in 29 of 30 days, which the statistics of Section~\ref{sec:methods:statistics} take into account.

\subsection{Model Protocol}
\label{sec:methods:model}

The main model is OpenAI's \ident{gpt-6-luna}, called through the Batch API (\path{/v1/chat/completions}, 24-hour completion window) in chunks of at most 1{,}500 requests. The model replaces the retired o4-mini; its API alias carries no dated snapshot, so the creation timestamp of the model object (2026-09-14) and the response identifier of every answer are stored. Requests use \ident{reasoning_effort} = \ident{medium}, the effort at which o4-mini ran in the published run, and the prompt text is unchanged from the Phase 4A run (prompt version \ident{v2.0_postgresql}); only the two dated arms of Section~\ref{sec:methods:arms} differ in the data lines, whose day labels are ISO dates. The wording of the prompt uses ``$>$70\%'' and ``$>$\$5B'', whereas the rule uses ``at least 21 of 30 days'' and ``$\geq$ \$5 billion''; the prompt is unchanged from the published run. The answer has nine fields (\ident{regime_detected}, \ident{regime_type}, \ident{positive_days}, \ident{negative_days}, \ident{avg_magnitude_billions}, \ident{sign_flips}, \ident{persistence_pct}, \ident{confidence} and \ident{reasoning}); the prompt, the schema and the request body are in Section~S6 of the Supplementary Materials, and the deposit (Appendix~\ref{app:data}) holds the input and the answer of every window. The one protocol change that bears on the answer is that these fields are enforced with a strict JSON schema, whereas the earlier runs requested JSON in the prompt text only; the change is disclosed here and does not alter the question asked. The published requests supplied neither a temperature nor a token limit (the AIAI Methods text states 1.0 and 16{,}384 tokens); the requests to gpt-6-luna supply no temperature either. They do cap completion at 4{,}000 tokens, reasoning tokens included, as a safety limit that no answer reached. An answer is read as JSON; a second attempt tolerates raw control characters inside strings (one Phase 4A answer needed it), a last fallback extracts the JSON object from free text, and the parse status of every answer is stored.

The bridge carries a gate. Cohen's kappa between \ident{bridge_jan} and the 1{,}412 published o4-mini answers has to be at least about 0.6, the value of the project checklist. The observed kappa is 0.99 (99.6\% agreement: 646 windows detected by both models, 5 by gpt-6-luna only, 0 by o4-mini only, 761 by neither), so the gate is passed. A full repeat of \ident{bridge_jan} gives a kappa of 0.98 against the first pass (1{,}476 pairs), which sets the scale of the run-to-run noise.

\subsection{Experimental Arms}
\label{sec:methods:arms}

Table~\ref{tab:arms} lists the arms. The arm \ident{r1_o4mini} holds the published o4-mini answers, backfilled from the surviving Phase 4A output file without an API call; 64 of its 1{,}476 answers were lost to a parsing error in January 2026 and are re-parsed from the stored raw output. Every headline number uses the 1{,}412 answers of the published result; the 64 re-parsed answers are reported separately (as \ident{r1_o4mini_all}). The arms \ident{r1_p3_o4mini} and \ident{r1_p4_o4mini} are backfilled in the same way from the 223 stored answers of each November 2025 baseline run. The arm \ident{bridge_jan} sends the exact published inputs to gpt-6-luna, which separates the effect of the model from the effect of the inputs. The other arms run gpt-6-luna on the complete series of each symbol; the ex-0DTE arms of QQQ and IWM start in 2020. The intraday arm uses composite windows whose last day is the late-session snapshot.

Two further groups of arms test the earlier results more directly. The Phase 3 (2024) and Phase 4 (2020) baselines of the earlier studies \cite{regan2026regimes,regan2026jrfm}, the 81.2\% versus 12.1\% contrast, were run on o4-mini in November 2025 with ISO calendar dates as day labels instead of relative labels. The arms \ident{p34_dated_2024} and \ident{p34_dated_2020} send the same 252 values per year, with the dates that were actually sent, to gpt-6-luna; the arms \ident{p34_obf_2024} and \ident{p34_obf_2020} send the identical values with Day-T labels. Within a year the two arms differ in the labels only, which isolates the effect of visible dates, and each is compared with the stored o4-mini answers. The two baselines are not on one convention (Table~\ref{tab:series}): the 2024 series is a gross call-plus-put magnitude with a negative sign imposed and the 2020 series is a magnitude without sign. We keep them verbatim because the aim is to replay the published contrast, which therefore cannot be read as one between a negative and a positive regime.

With the thresholds fixed in dollars, QQQ and IWM have no persistent window (Section~S3 of the Supplementary Materials), so the unscaled arms only test whether the model also rejects every window. As a disclosed protocol change, the arms \ident{full_qqq_scaled} and \ident{full_iwm_scaled} therefore run on \ident{new_full_scaled}: the \ident{new_full} values of QQQ and IWM multiplied by one fixed factor per symbol, the median over 2020-01-01 to 2026-10-09 of SPY's volume-weighted open-interest notional divided by the symbol's (\ident{total_weighted_oi} $\times$ 100 $\times$ underlying price, from \path{gex.options_daily_summary}; the quantity is built from volume times open interest, not from open interest alone; Section~S1 of the Supplementary Materials). % src: report.yaml diagnostics.scaling new_full_scaled:QQQ / :IWM (factor; n_days_in_factor now 1700 and 1701)

\subsection{Statistics}
\label{sec:methods:statistics}

Detection rates are reported with 95\% Wilson intervals \cite{brown2001interval}, and differences between rates with Newcombe--Wilson intervals \cite{newcombe1998interval}. Windows have stride one, so neighbouring windows share 29 of their 30 days and cannot be treated as independent. The primary interval for the rate of 2024 and 2025 and for the shift between periods is a moving-block bootstrap with blocks of 60 consecutive windows (95\% percentile interval, 5{,}000 draws, seed 261); other block lengths and an episode bootstrap are in Section~S9 of the Supplementary Materials. Every p-value is accompanied by two secondary checks: (i)~a calendar-quarter block bootstrap \cite{kunsch1989jackknife}, computed by enumerating all equally likely resamples when they are few (up to ten quarters, or for two independent periods up to 200{,}000 distinct pairs of resamples) and from 2{,}000 draws (seed 261) otherwise and (ii)~the same statistic on a non-overlapping 30-day subsample, the greedy chain of windows that share no day, starting at the first window of the period. We make no adjustment for multiplicity across arms, periods, series and thresholds. Agreement between models is measured with Cohen's kappa \cite{cohen1960coefficient}, and paired detection rates across series or arms with the exact McNemar test \cite{mcnemar1947note} (on the subsample; the stride-one value is reported as anti-conservative); effect sizes between years use $\varphi$. % src: stored_analyses.yaml parameters (seed 261, n_boot 5000, primary_block 60, block_lengths 30 to 120)
% src: stats.yaml meta (bootstrap_quarter, nonoverlap_30d, period_shift); rates entry labeller=classifier series_key=new_full symbol=SPY period=2024-2025 (nonoverlap_30d: k, n, rate_pct, rate_pct_range_over_starts)
The p-values of the AIAI and JRFM papers \cite{regan2026regimes,regan2026jrfm} were computed under independence of windows. The quarter-block bootstrap resamples the calendar quarters of the window end with replacement, and a paired contrast resamples the same quarters for both series. Its two-sided p-value is $2\min\{P(d\le 0),P(d\ge 0)\}$, bounded below by the probability of one resample ($1/k^k$ for $k$ quarters enumerated exactly, the product $1/(k_0^{k_0}k_1^{k_1})$ for two independent periods, and $1/2{,}001$ for draws). With $k$ quarters there are $k^k$ equally likely resamples, so the interval of a single year (four quarters, 256 resamples) is coarse. Enumeration makes the resampling distribution exact and independent of the seed; it does not make the coverage of the interval exact, which with four to eight blocks is not known to be nominal. A p-value that equals the bound above is printed as $p\le$ bound: it says that no resample reached the observed difference, not how small the p-value is, and for two series on the same windows it adds nothing to the difference itself. The period 2024 to 2025 gives 8 quarters. The non-overlapping subsample depends on its first window, so the 30 possible first windows give 30 chains, and the range of their rates is reported beside the rate of the chain that starts at the first window. For the rule on \ident{new_full} (SPY, window ends in 2024 and 2025), the chain holds 3 of 17 windows (17.6\%), and the rate ranges from 5.9\% to 25.0\% across the starts. Between periods, $\varphi$ is signed (late minus early) and the p-value comes from Fisher's exact test. Each p-value in Section~\ref{sec:results} names the method it uses.
