\subsection{Regime Criteria}
\label{sec:methods:criteria}

A 30-trading-day window is a persistent regime when three criteria hold \cite{regan2026regimes,regan2026jrfm}: (i)~persistence, at least 21 of the 30 days (70\%) share the sign of net GEX; (ii)~magnitude, the mean absolute net GEX is at least \$5~billion; and (iii)~stability, the sign changes at most five times. The deterministic classifier (the rule) labels every other window as low conviction (a persistent sign as in (i) and a mean magnitude of at least \$3~billion, but failing (ii) or (iii)) or transitional. The detection label used throughout is the binary persistent or not-persistent verdict (\ident{regime_detected} for the model, \ident{clf_should_detect} for the rule). The thresholds are those of the earlier studies and are not re-tuned.
% src: src/validation/regime_classifier.py (positive days are values > 0; classification order); report.yaml diagnostics.thresholds
Zero GEX counts as non-positive. A window with a persistent sign and a mean magnitude of at least \$5~billion but more than five sign changes fails the stability criterion and is labelled low conviction, because its mean still exceeds \$3~billion. The prompt shows values rounded to 0.01B, whereas the rule replays the unrounded USD values stored with each window. On 4 of the 38{,}592 windows the rounded values would give another four-way label, and on two of them (scaled QQQ, window ends 2021-03-05 and 2021-03-08) another verdict. The thresholds are fixed in dollars before the runs, for every symbol. This choice has a consequence for QQQ and IWM, whose gamma is smaller than that of SPY, and Section~\ref{sec:results:cross} reports the resulting floor effect.

\subsection{Daily GEX Series}
\label{sec:methods:series}

For each symbol and trading day $t$ the net dealer gamma exposure is
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{GEX}_t=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{C}_t} s_i\,\Gamma_i\,\mathrm{OI}_i\cdot 100\cdot S_t^{2}\cdot 0.01,
\qquad s_i=\begin{cases}+1 & \text{call,}\\ -1 & \text{put,}\end{cases}
\label{eq:gex}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{C}_t$ is the set of contracts listed on day $t$ with a defined gamma $\Gamma_i$, positive open interest $\mathrm{OI}_i$ and a positive underlying price $S_t$. The series of Table~\ref{tab:series} differ in how $\Gamma_i$ is obtained, in which expiries and legs enter $\mathcal{C}_t$, and in where the values come from.

\subsection{Windows and Deterministic Classifier}
\label{sec:methods:windows}

A window consists of 30 consecutive rows of a series (days $T{-}29$ to $T{+}0$), with one window per end date (stride one) and end dates up to 2026-10-09. A span that covers a calendar gap of more than four days is flagged (a gap of five or more days is not a holiday weekend). The exchange closure of 2012-10-29 and 2012-10-30 (Hurricane Sandy) is such a gap without a missing trading day, so in each series that covers 2012 (\ident{new_full} and \ident{new_ex0dte} for the three symbols, and \ident{old_bs}) the 29 windows that span it are flagged although they are complete. The number of trading days of the symbol's master calendar (\path{gex.options_daily_summary}) that are absent from the series inside the span is stored with the window. A third detector compares the span with the NYSE trading calendar and stores the number of NYSE trading days that the series lacks inside it (\ident{nyse_missing_days}); it finds a single day that is absent from the series and from the master calendar alike, which the first two detectors cannot see. A flag marks a window but does not drop it, and flagged windows are kept in every rate.

The gap detector fires on two spans of the complete series, the closure of 2012-10-29 and 2012-10-30, when no day is missing, and the absence of 2019-09-03; the NYSE detector fires on 2018-11-06, 2019-09-03 and 2020-06-23 for all three symbols and on 2024-09-30 for QQQ. Values are relabelled ``Day T-29'' to ``Day T+0'' with no dates or tickers, exactly as in Phase 4A, except in the two dated arms of Section~\ref{sec:methods:arms}, which carry the ISO dates of the November 2025 prompts. The rule of Section~\ref{sec:methods:criteria} labels every window independently of any model call, and its verdict is the reference label for each arm. The windows that end in 2026, up to 2026-10-09, form the post-publication period (Section~\ref{sec:results:post}): the published sample ends on 2025-12-31, so the 29 earliest of these windows still contain days of it, and those with ends from 2026-02-13 lie entirely after it.

The January inputs and the four Phase 3 and 4 inputs keep their own calendars (rule J), and a build stops unless it reproduces the surviving batch file byte for byte. \subsection{Model Protocol}
\label{sec:methods:model}

The main model is OpenAI's \ident{gpt-6-luna}, called through the Batch API (\path{/v1/chat/completions}, 24-hour completion window) in chunks of at most 1{,}500 requests. The model replaces the retired o4-mini; its API alias carries no dated snapshot, so the creation timestamp of the model object (2026-09-14) and the response identifier of every answer are stored. Requests use \ident{reasoning_effort} = \ident{medium}, the effort at which o4-mini ran in the published run, and the prompt text is unchanged from the Phase 4A run (prompt version \ident{v2.0_postgresql}); only the two dated arms of Section~\ref{sec:methods:arms} differ in the data lines, whose day labels are ISO dates. The wording of the prompt uses ``$>$70\%'' and ``$>$\$5B'', whereas the rule uses ``at least 21 of 30 days'' and ``$\geq$ \$5 billion''; the prompt is unchanged from the published run. The answer has nine fields (Appendix~\ref{app:prompt}). The one protocol change that bears on the answer is that these fields are enforced with a strict JSON schema, whereas the earlier runs requested JSON in the prompt text only; the change is disclosed here and does not alter the question asked. The published requests supplied neither a temperature nor a token limit (the AIAI Methods text states 1.0 and 16{,}384 tokens); the requests to gpt-6-luna supply no temperature either. They do cap completion at 4{,}000 tokens, reasoning tokens included, as a safety limit that no answer reached. An answer is read as JSON; a second attempt tolerates raw control characters inside strings (one Phase 4A answer needed it), a last fallback extracts the JSON object from free text, and the parse status of every answer is stored.

A third model answers seven arms (Section~\ref{sec:methods:arms}). The alias gpt-6-luna can be retired as o4-mini was, so these arms use a model whose weights can be run again: gpt-oss-20b \cite{openai2025gptoss}, an open-weights model released by OpenAI under the Apache-2.0 licence, at the fixed revision \texttt{6cee5e81} of its public repository \path{openai/gpt-oss-20b}. It runs on the authors' NVIDIA A100 GPUs with the offline batch runner of vLLM~0.32.0 \cite{kwon2023vllm}, which reads request files in the format of the Batch API, and the weights are read from a local copy of that revision without network access. The request body is that of gpt-6-luna with only the model name changed: the same prompt, \ident{reasoning_effort} = \ident{medium}, the strict JSON schema and the limit of 4{,}000 completion tokens, with one more request at 8{,}000 tokens for an answer cut off at the limit. vLLM applies the schema through its structured-output decoding once the reasoning channel of the model has ended. No temperature is sent, so the engine samples at its default of 1.0, with the engine seed fixed at 261; answers still need not repeat exactly, because requests share the engine in batches whose composition varies. \subsection{Statistics}
\label{sec:methods:statistics}

Detection rates are reported with 95\% Wilson intervals \cite{brown2001interval}, and differences between rates with Newcombe--Wilson intervals \cite{newcombe1998interval}. Windows have stride one, so neighbouring windows share 29 of their 30 days and cannot be treated as independent. Every p-value is therefore accompanied by (i)~a calendar-quarter block bootstrap \cite{kunsch1989jackknife}, computed exactly over all equally likely resamples when they are few (up to ten quarters, or for two independent periods up to 200{,}000 distinct pairs of resamples) and from 2{,}000 draws (seed 261) otherwise and (ii)~the same statistic on a non-overlapping 30-day subsample, the greedy chain of windows that share no day, starting at the first window of the period. Agreement between models is measured with Cohen's kappa \cite{cohen1960coefficient}, and paired detection rates across series or arms with the exact McNemar test \cite{mcnemar1947note} (on the subsample; the stride-one value is reported as anti-conservative); effect sizes between years use $\varphi$.
% src: stats.yaml meta (bootstrap_quarter, nonoverlap_30d, period_shift); rates entry labeller=classifier series_key=new_full symbol=SPY period=2024-2025 (nonoverlap_30d: k, n, rate_pct, rate_pct_range_over_starts)
The p-values of the AIAI and JRFM papers \cite{regan2026regimes,regan2026jrfm} were computed under independence of windows. The quarter-block bootstrap resamples the calendar quarters of the window end with replacement, and a paired contrast resamples the same quarters for both series. Its two-sided p-value is $2\min\{P(d\le 0),P(d\ge 0)\}$, bounded below by the probability of one resample ($1/k^k$ for $k$ quarters enumerated exactly, the product $1/(k_0^{k_0}k_1^{k_1})$ for two independent periods, and $1/2{,}001$ for draws). With $k$ quarters there are $k^k$ equally likely resamples, so the interval of a single year (four quarters, 256 resamples) is coarse; enumerating the resamples makes it exact and independent of the seed. The period 2024 to 2025 gives 8 quarters. The non-overlapping subsample depends on its first window, so the 30 possible first windows give 30 chains, and the range of their rates is reported beside the rate of the chain that starts at the first window. For the rule on \ident{new_full} (SPY, window ends in 2024 and 2025), the chain holds 3 of 17 windows (17.6\%), and the rate ranges from 5.9\% to 25.0\% across the starts. Between periods, $\varphi$ is signed (late minus early) and the p-value comes from Fisher's exact test. Each p-value in Section~\ref{sec:results} names the method it uses.
