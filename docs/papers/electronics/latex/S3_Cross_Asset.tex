\section{Cross-Asset Replication and the Floor Effect}
\label{sup:cross}

Table~\ref{tab:cross}, Table~\ref{tab:s:llm} and Figure~\ref{fig:cross} repeat the analysis of Section~4 of the article for QQQ and IWM. The fixed dollar thresholds of Section~3.1 of the article can produce a floor effect for smaller underlyings, which we report; the scaled series of Section~3.6 of the article show whether the model's verdict still follows the criteria when the floor is lifted.
% src: report.yaml diagnostics.floor_effect (max_window_mean_abs_gex_bn) and diagnostics.floor_effect_note; detection_by_year.csv scope=all (QQQ, IWM, SPY); windows.csv.gz (clf_persistence_pct, clf_sign_flips, clf_avg_magnitude)
The floor effect is complete. Under the fixed thresholds the rule detects no window of QQQ (0 of 3{,}878, windows ending 2011 to 2026-10-09) and none of IWM (0 of 4{,}691, 2008 to 2026-10-09), in any year and on either series. The largest 30-day mean $|\mathrm{GEX}|$ is \$4.92~billion for QQQ and \$4.98~billion for IWM on \ident{new_full} (\$4.11~billion and \$4.37~billion without same-day expiries), against \$16.00~billion for SPY, so no window reaches the \$5~billion criterion. This is a consequence of the fixed thresholds, a design decision fixed before the runs, and not a modelling result. The magnitude criterion is the binding one: the persistence and sign-flip tests alone pass 1{,}428 of 3{,}878 windows of QQQ (36.8\%) and 3{,}369 of 4{,}691 of IWM (71.8\%), against 1{,}775 of 4{,}691 windows of SPY (37.8\%). gpt-6-luna labels no window of QQQ or IWM as persistent either (0 of 3{,}878 and 0 of 4{,}691 on \ident{new_full}; 0 of 1{,}700 and 0 of 1{,}701 on \ident{new_ex0dte}), so the unscaled arms show that the model also rejects every window; they cannot test its discrimination.

% src: report.yaml diagnostics.scaling (factor 4.224724 and 11.43094 shown as 4.22 and 11.43, n_days_in_factor 1699 and 1700 as committed in a996099 (the build of the fixed factors; the current file holds the recomputed 1700 and 1701) at the build of 2026-10-29; ratio_p25 7.727908 for IWM); series parquet new_full_scaled_QQQ / _IWM series_metadata (ratio_min, ratio_max, ratio_p25, ratio_p75); stats.yaml contrasts series_contrast family scale and period_shift for new_full_scaled
The scaled variant multiplies the \ident{new_full} values by one factor per symbol: 4.22 for QQQ (median of 1{,}699 daily ratios) and 11.43 for IWM (1{,}700 days). It is a disclosed protocol change (Section~3.6 of the article). With it the rule detects 351 of 1{,}671 windows of QQQ (21.0\%) and 902 of 1{,}672 windows of IWM (53.9\%), and gpt-6-luna 361 (21.6\%) and 905 (54.1\%). The model follows the rule on the scaled inputs: the verdicts agree in 99.4\% of the QQQ windows (kappa 0.982) and 99.7\% of the IWM windows (kappa 0.994). The paired difference to the unscaled arms is 21.0 percentage points for QQQ (quarter-block bootstrap interval [12.7, 31.2], $p\le5.0\times10^{-4}$, the floor of 2{,}000 draws; exact McNemar test on the non-overlapping subsample $p=4.9\times10^{-4}$) and 53.9 for IWM ([40.7, 67.4], $p\le5.0\times10^{-4}$; $p=4.7\times10^{-10}$); for the model the differences are 21.6 and 54.1 percentage points.

The scaled rates depend on the factor and are not a measurement of QQQ or IWM regimes. The ratio of the volume-weighted open-interest notional of SPY to that of the symbol is not constant: for QQQ the daily ratio runs from 0.78 to 38.32 (quartiles 3.30 and 5.73), and for IWM from 1.51 to 110.57 (quartiles 7.73 and 16.46). A single factor puts the exposure on the scale of SPY on a typical day, no more, and another factor would give other rates. The factor is built from volume times open interest and was decided after the zero floor had been seen (Section~3.6 of the article). The scaled arms therefore show only that the model follows the criteria once the floor is lifted. They show no step after 2023: the rate of the rule on the scaled IWM series is 65.2\% in 2020 to 2023 and 35.7\% in 2024 and 2025 (shift $-$29.5 percentage points, $p=0.061$ by bootstrap, $p=0.137$ by Fisher exact test on the subsample), and on the scaled QQQ series 25.9\% and 13.4\% ($-$12.5 percentage points, $p=0.170$, $p=0.292$).

In the 194 windows that end in 2026 (Section~4.7 of the article) the unscaled series give no detection by the rule or by the model for QQQ and IWM (0 of 194 windows each). On the scaled series the rule detects 16.0\% (QQQ) and 44.8\% (IWM) of these windows and the model 16.5\% and 45.4\%.
