\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

A language model that labels a market regime may be recalling the period it was trained on, or it may be applying the criteria it is shown to the numbers it is shown. Temporal obfuscation removes dates and tickers, so that the model has only the values and the stated criteria. The test is only as sound as the numerical series that it shows to the model. This article re-runs the published 30-day regime test on complete inputs, that is, on daily series that hold both the call leg and the put leg of the options chain, and asks two questions. Does the published regime result hold on complete inputs? And does a current hosted model, gpt-6-luna, reproduce the answers of the retired model that gave the published result? The language-model arms test whether the model applies the criteria that the prompt states. The rule is in the prompt, and the reference label of every window is that rule, so these arms cannot separate recall from reasoning, and the article draws no conclusion on that distinction.

\subsection{Motivation: Data-Definition Drift and Model Retirement}
\label{sec:introduction:motivation}

The published run used o4-mini in January 2026. The model no longer answers inference requests (the endpoint returns \ident{model_not_found}). The raw options table was reloaded on 2026-02-03, and the daily GEX table was rebuilt on 2026-10-08 from stored per-contract gamma, with same-day expiries included (Section~\ref{sec:methods:lineage}). A result that depends on a model and on an input definition that can both disappear cannot be re-run, only re-interpreted. The study therefore recovers the exact inputs of the published run from the stored batch file, bridges a second model (gpt-6-luna) to o4-mini on these inputs, and stores every input, prompt and response. A hosted alias can be retired as well; a companion manuscript \cite{regan2026openweights} therefore repeats seven of these arms with open-weights models at fixed revisions on local hardware. This article reports none of those results, and the data deposit that the two papers share holds those answers as well (Appendix~\ref{app:data}).

\subsection{Relation to Prior Work}
\label{sec:introduction:relation}

This article is the extended version of the AIAI~2026 conference paper \cite{regan2026regimes}, which introduced 30-day regime detection under temporal obfuscation and built on the single-day study presented at IEEE BigData~2025 \cite{regan2025obfuscation}. Both lines of work are combined in a separate journal article \cite{regan2026jrfm} (Journal of Risk and Financial Management, JRFM).

Relative to all three publications, the new elements are:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=*,itemsep=0pt]
\item the provenance and definition of the daily input series;
\item a second model, gpt-6-luna, bridged to o4-mini on identical inputs;
\item the years 2008--2019 and 2026, and, in the Supplementary Materials, the exchange-traded funds (ETFs) QQQ (Nasdaq-100) and IWM (Russell 2000) next to SPY (S\&P 500) and an intraday check;
\item overlap-aware inference;
\item an obfuscated-versus-dated rerun of the Phase~3 and~4 baselines; and
\item a disclosed per-symbol scaling for QQQ and IWM (Supplementary Materials).
\end{enumerate}
The recap of the earlier design is limited to Section~\ref{sec:methods}. Nothing that is reported as new here is reported as new in the companion manuscript.

\subsection{Research Questions}
\label{sec:introduction:rqs}

We address six questions:
\begin{enumerate}[label=RQ\arabic*.,leftmargin=*]
\item Which daily series were the published inputs, and how do the deterministic labels change on the complete series?
\item Does gpt-6-luna reproduce the o4-mini labels on the exact published inputs?
\item Does the model's regime call follow the input series and the criteria that the prompt states?
\item How much of the persistence signal lives in zero-days-to-expiration (0DTE), i.e.\ same-day-expiry, gamma?
\item Do the answers hold for 2008--2019 and in the post-publication period (2026)?
\item Does the 2024-versus-2020 contrast of the earlier baseline runs, whose prompts carried calendar dates, persist when the dates are replaced by relative day labels?
\end{enumerate}
RQ1 is answered in Sections~\ref{sec:results:inputs}--\ref{sec:results:replay}, RQ2 in Section~\ref{sec:results:bridge}, RQ3 in Sections~\ref{sec:results:complete} and~\ref{sec:results:fidelity}, RQ4 in Section~\ref{sec:results:ablation}, RQ5 in Sections~\ref{sec:results:deep} and~\ref{sec:results:post} and RQ6 in Section~\ref{sec:results:dated}. The cross-asset replication for QQQ and IWM and the late-session intraday check are in the Supplementary Materials (Sections~S3 and~S4).

This article makes six contributions.
(i)~It reconstructs the published inputs and recomputes the deterministic labels on the complete series: on the published inputs the rule detects every window of 2024 and 2025, on the complete series 12.0\% (Sections~\ref{sec:results:inputs} and~\ref{sec:results:replay}).
(ii)~It analyses the sensitivity of the deterministic labels to the series definition across six daily series, one of them a call-only diagnostic, and 2008--2026. Removing same-day expiries does not restore the published rates (Sections~\ref{sec:results:ablation} and~\ref{sec:results:deep}).
(iii)~It bridges o4-mini and gpt-6-luna on identical inputs. The two models give nearly the same verdict on the published windows, and on the complete series gpt-6-luna follows the rule almost everywhere, which shows rule-following on stated criteria (Sections~\ref{sec:results:bridge}, \ref{sec:results:complete} and~\ref{sec:results:fidelity}).
(iv)~It applies overlap-aware statistics to stride-one windows: the quarter-block bootstrap and a non-overlapping subsample next to the Wilson intervals. The shift of the pooled rule rate after 2023 is stated with its interval, and the data give no evidence of a rise at the nominal rule (Section~\ref{sec:methods:statistics} for the method, Section~\ref{sec:results:replay} for the values).
(v)~It stores every input, prompt and response of the hosted and backfilled arms in result tables (36{,}706 answers), from which each table and figure of this article is generated (Section~\ref{sec:methods:storage}).
(vi)~It reruns the Phase~3 and~4 baselines with relative day labels instead of calendar dates. For 2024, gpt-6-luna detects 180 of 223 windows (80.7\%) with relative labels and 181 of 223 (81.2\%) with dates, so we find no detectable difference; the comparison has no positive control and is not a test of equivalence (Section~\ref{sec:results:dated}).
