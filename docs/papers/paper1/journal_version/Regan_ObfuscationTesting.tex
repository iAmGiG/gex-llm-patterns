\begin{abstract}
Standard gamma exposure (GEX) metrics compress rich options chain data into scalar values, potentially discarding structural signal critical for detecting dealer hedging constraints. We demonstrate that large language models processing raw strike-level data—open interest, implied volatility, and bid-ask spreads per strike—achieve 92.3\% detection of dealer hedging patterns, outperforming identical models given pre-calculated Net GEX by 30.8 percentage points. This finding establishes that unstructured feature extraction recovers signal lost in parametric compression of the options surface.

We validate detection quality through obfuscation testing, a methodology that strips all temporal context (dates, tickers, market events) while preserving structural relationships. Across 242 trading days of S\&P 500 options data, 91.2\% of detected patterns materialize in subsequent price movements. The WHO→WHOM→WHAT causal framework requires articulation of economic actors (dealers), affected parties (traders), and forced mechanisms (pro-cyclical hedging), ensuring detected patterns reflect genuine market microstructure rather than statistical artifacts.

Five validation tests establish signal integrity. First, detection capability remains stable (68--74\% quarterly) while trading profitability collapses (Sharpe 1.8 → 0.1), establishing these as risk management signals rather than alpha generators. Second, non-detection days exhibit significantly fragmented gamma distribution (p < 0.0001, Cohen's d = 0.68), proving sensitivity to signal concentration rather than base-rate guessing. Third, detection days show 33\% lower intraday range expansion than baseline (p = 0.03), confirming identification of volatility \textit{suppression} mechanisms—inverse p-hacking that cannot result from fishing for high-volatility outcomes. Fourth, statistical baseline achieves AUC = 0.681 predicting T+1 outcomes from end-of-day features, validating latent information content. Fifth, raw chain superiority establishes that standard industry metrics (Net GEX) represent lossy compression of actionable structural information. Our methodology provides rigorous validation standards for deploying AI-based structural analysis in financial risk systems.
\end{abstract}
