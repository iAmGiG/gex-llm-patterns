\begin{abstract}
We present a validation framework for large language model (LLM) structural reasoning applied to financial market regime detection. A critical challenge in deploying LLMs for domain-specific analysis is distinguishing genuine structural reasoning from training data memorization---a concern that intensifies as models encounter domain-specific data during pre-training. We address this through \textbf{temporal obfuscation testing}: systematically stripping calendar dates, ticker symbols, and contextual markers from input sequences, forcing the model to reason purely from numerical structure. Applying this framework to 30-day gamma exposure (GEX) sequences---a measure of aggregate options dealer positioning constraints derived from open interest data---we validate detection across five phases spanning 2020--2025 (1,412 real windows plus 809 synthetic controls, totaling 2,221 evaluations). The framework achieves 81.2\% detection of persistent dealer regimes in 2024 versus 12.1\% in 2020 (69.1 percentage point separation, $\varphi = 0.672$, $p < 0.0001$), with 0\% false positives on transitional and low-magnitude negative controls. Multi-year analysis reveals gradual regime evolution tracking zero-days-to-expiration (0DTE) options adoption---from 3.7\% detection in 2021 to 100\% in 2024---rather than sharp structural breaks, with GEX magnitude growing 360\% (\$5B to \$23B). These results validate LLM structural reasoning capabilities in a challenging quantitative domain while demonstrating temporal obfuscation as a generalizable methodology for preventing training data contamination in domain-specific AI applications.

\end{abstract}
